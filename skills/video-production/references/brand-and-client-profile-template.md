# Brand and client profile: template

The first document of the video pack. File it in the project as
`docs/video/01-brand-and-client-profile.md`.

It is the brief. Everything built later (the concept, the script, the prompts, the
page the video sits on) is checked against the positioning and tone sections here.
A concept that cannot be traced back to a line in this document is decoration.

## How to use this template

- Fill every section. A section with nothing to say gets one line saying why, never
  a silent gap.
- Write in English for the agents who read it. Quote the client's own copy verbatim,
  in the language of the site, wherever the copy itself is the evidence.
- Separate three kinds of statement and mark which is which: **observed** (seen on
  the site, in a file, in a photo), **said** (the client's own words, quoted) and
  **inferred** (your reading). The source pack this template comes from did this in
  its headings ("as it exists on the site", "as it should be read for all new work",
  "inferred from the service list").
- Numbers and contact details are copied from the source, never typed from memory.
- Anything only partly legible in a screenshot or scan is flagged `[uncertain]` and
  verified against the live source before it is reused.
- Text in `<angle brackets>` is a slot to fill. Delete the instruction lines when
  the section is written.

## Companion document: the verbatim content extraction

When the client already has a site, write a second file beside this one,
`docs/video/02-site-content-extraction.md`: every text visible on the current site,
page by page and section by section, verbatim, with English labels. It is the single
source of truth for existing copy. This profile then quotes from it and never
paraphrases site copy from memory. A client with no site skips it and says so in the
pack's README.

## Pack README: the "key facts at a glance" block

The pack's `00-README.md` opens with one line on the client, one on the engagement
(what is being built and in which language and direction), the date, a table of the
files in the pack with purpose and language, a "how to use this pack" list, and a
short fact block. The fact block repeats, without elaboration, the things an agent
needs in the first ten seconds:

- Brand name and tagline as they appear in the logo
- Owner or face of the firm, with title
- The current hero headline and the primary call-to-action label, verbatim
- The form button label, verbatim
- The platform the current site runs on
- The palette in one line (logo colours and the site's band or overlay colour)
- The metaphor or image world the current site is already committed to, if any

Contact details live in the project's own files, not in the kit.

# 01: Brand and client profile

## 1. Identity

Fill each line from the logo files, the site footer and the client's own words.

- **Business name:** `<as registered and as displayed; note if they differ>`
- **Logo sub-line / descriptor:** `<verbatim>`
- **Logo name line:** `<if the standalone logo carries a different name line>`
- **Owner / face of the firm:** `<name, title, role>`
- **Contact channels:** `<which channels exist: phone, messaging, form, chat; the
  details themselves are quoted from the project's facts file>`
- **Geography:** `<where the practice sits, which place the site targets for search,
  and the real scope of the work: local, regional, national>`. Say so when the search
  target is narrower than the real scope.
- **Platform of the current site:** `<the builder or CMS, and how you know>`

Close the section with one paragraph on **who is speaking**. Is the business a
single practitioner presented as a firm? Does the copy use "we" for process and "I"
for values? Is that dual voice deliberate? State whether new work preserves it. This
decides who appears in the video (a person, hands only, or nobody) and whose voice
any on-screen line carries.

## 2. What the client does

One sentence on the profession and the specialism, with the thing that sets this
practice apart from the routine version of the same profession.

Then the full service list, **grouped into tiers by the client's own emphasis**
(what appears in the navigation, in the hero, in the footer), not by your own
taxonomy:

- **Tier 1: flagship.** The services that appear in the navigation and the hero.
- **Tier 2: specialist.** Niche or context-bound work; note any service that has
  its own long article or page.
- **Tier 3: transactional or supporting.** Everything else.

Each service gets its name verbatim and a short English gloss.

End with **the through-line**: one bolded sentence that says what every tier has in
common, stated as what the work does to the customer's situation, not as a list of
qualities. This sentence is the most important line of the profile. The video's
metaphor must be a true picture of it. If no single through-line exists, say so;
that is a finding, and a video built on a forced one will read as decoration.

Method note: a strong through-line is often a statement of the form "the work
changes X that someone else has already set". A line like that is what makes a
chosen metaphor accurate rather than pretty. Look for the equivalent: the real
product underneath the service list.

## 3. Positioning

Three blocks, in this order.

**Current positioning statements (verbatim).** The hero headline and sub-line, any
slogan-like header, the founder's closing line. Quoted exactly, with where each
appears.

**Positioning as it should be read for all new work.** One blockquote, two
sentences at most, in English. This is your reading, marked as such. It is the
sentence the concept pitch will be tested against.

**Value pillars, in the client's own vocabulary.** A numbered list of the words the
client actually uses, each with its gloss and where it recurs (hero, about, founder
section, process steps, testimonials header). Rank by how often the copy returns to
the word. Recurrence across sections is the evidence; one mention is not a pillar.

**What the client is not positioning on.** Name the claims the copy deliberately
avoids (price, speed, volume and so on) and write "do not introduce these". This
line protects the video from the wrong promise.

## 4. Audiences

State first whether the site segments its audiences explicitly or whether the list
below is inferred, and from what (service list, article content, calls to action).

Then a numbered list. Each audience gets:

- a bolded name in plain words (who they are in relation to the service)
- the situation that brings them (what arrived in the post, what decision is due)
- their state (motivated, time-bound, anxious, comparing, sent by someone else)
- what they value in this provider
- which existing element of the site is aimed at them, if any
- whether they are a client, a referrer, or both

Close with **the common thread**: what is true in every audience's situation, and
the emotional need underneath it, in one sentence. The video is watched by all of
them without sound, so it can only speak to the common thread.

## 5. Tone of voice

**As it exists on the site.** Observations, each one checkable against the copy:

- register (formal, plain, colloquial), slang, punctuation habits
- sentence shape in marketing sections versus long-form sections
- the abstract nouns the copy leans on
- how promises are framed: as process or as outcome; whether any result is ever
  promised
- which section carries the warmest and most specific writing; that is the voice to
  extend

**Guidance for new copy.** Short imperative rules derived from the observations:

- what to keep, and why this audience expects it
- what to replace (for example, abstract quality nouns with concrete process verbs;
  list the verbs). Note whether the candidate metaphor supplies those verbs. A
  metaphor that gives the copy a set of concrete verbs is doing real work.
- person and number for the founder and for the firm
- what is never promised
- language rules: the one language of the site, and how any second-language gesture
  in the current design is treated (a single brand gesture or a system; never a
  half-pattern)

## 6. Visual identity

**Logo.** Describe it as shapes and movement, not as a file name: what the mark is,
how it travels across the frame, the lettering beneath it, any secondary line or
rule. Then name **the single most reusable motif** in it (a curve, a ribbon, a grid,
a cut corner) in bold. The video's visual motif is built on this, so the description
must be precise enough for someone who has never seen the logo to draw the shape.

**Palette (sampled from the logo and the site).** One line per colour: role, where
it appears, the sampled value marked as approximate. Include the dark used for bands
and image overlays, the button colour, and the whites and off-whites. Sampled values
are observations; the project's tokens are the authority once they exist.

**Typography on the current site.** Body and heading faces (or the nearest known
family, marked as a likeness), any display face, any handwritten or script element.
Say what each signature gesture connects to (for example, a handwritten header that
echoes the founder's personal voice) and whether the video's end card can echo it.

**Imagery on the current site.** A bullet per image or image type: subject,
treatment (desaturated, tinted, overlaid), where it sits. Call out stylistic tics
(oversized display words, bilingual gestures) with a verdict: keep it as a system or
drop it on purpose, not half-keep.

**The person, physically (only when a character or avatar may be made).** One
paragraph, written from the supplied photographs, that would let a stranger pick the
person out: age range, complexion, hair, facial hair, glasses, eyes, expression,
build. Then wardrobe: what they wear on the site, what they wear in the supplied
stills, and which one brand work uses. This paragraph becomes the fixed `<CHARACTER>`
block of the production pipeline, so write it once and carefully. It is written only
after the person has agreed in writing to a generated likeness; without that, write
"no likeness: hands and silhouette only".

## 7. Current site structure (as observed)

**Global.** Header (logo position, navigation items in order, call-to-action,
contact icons), floating elements, any contact popup with its fields and button,
the footer's layout.

**Home page sections, in order.** A numbered list, one line per section: what it is,
the image or device it uses, the heading verbatim. Mark it "reconstructed" when it
comes from screenshots rather than the live page.

**Inner pages observed.** One line per page type seen: the hero treatment, the body
structure, which home-page sections repeat beneath it.

This section exists so the video's placement can be decided against a real page
order: which section it sits behind, what text sits on top of it, and what it must
not compete with.

## 8. Gaps and opportunities in the current site

A bullet list for the rebuild brief. Each bullet is a specific observation and a
specific consequence, not a general complaint. The source pack's list had these
kinds of entry; look for each:

- a metaphor or image world that is present in the pictures but **absent from the
  copy** (or the reverse), and where the rebuild should carry it lightly: section
  titles, process steps, the end-card line
- a flat list that hides the real hierarchy of services
- missing audience routing (an entry point that sends each audience to its page)
- strong content with weak hierarchy
- a display gesture that is half a system
- a signature element that is illegible at its display size
- whether the site has video today, and the role the new video is meant to play:
  a silent looping hero or section background that must not fight the headline
- content that is truncated or incomplete on the current site and where the full
  version can be fetched

## 9. Contact and conversion elements (verbatim labels)

Every label a visitor can act on, quoted exactly: the header button, section calls
to action, the lead band's title and sub-line, the form's field labels and button,
the popup's title and sub-line, messaging link text, chat bubble text. The page
around the video reuses these labels unchanged, and the social end card takes its
closing line from here.

## Check before the profile is used

- The through-line (section 2) is one sentence and is true of every tier.
- The positioning blockquote (section 3) is marked as your reading.
- Every verbatim quote matches the content extraction file character for character.
- The motif (section 6) is described well enough to draw.
- The person paragraph exists only with written consent, or the no-likeness line
  stands in its place.
- No contact detail or number was typed from memory.
