# Brand video concept and script: template

The creative document of the video pack. File it in the project as
`docs/video/03-brand-video-concept-and-script.md`. The execution document
(`04-production-pipeline.md`, written from `production-pipeline-fal.md`) refers to
beats by the numbers defined here, so the two are read together.

Sections 1 to 6 are what gets pitched to the Art Director, once. Section 7 is
written in full only after the written lock. No image or video is generated before
that lock.

## How to use this template

- Keep the section order and numbers. Other documents point at them.
- Text in `<angle brackets>` is a slot. Lines marked **Method** explain why a
  section exists and what makes it work; keep the rule, delete the explanation when
  the section is written.
- English for agent-facing explanation. The site's language for any on-screen text
  and for beat titles.
- A running example appears in boxes marked **Example**. It is invented for
  illustration and is not a past project: *a hand bookbinder standing for a small
  contract-law practice*. Replace all of it.

# 03: Brand video, concept and script

Working title: **`<title in the site's language>`** (English working title:
*`<title>`*)

**Method.** The working title should already contain the metaphor's key word and the
client's subject in one phrase. If the title needs explaining, the concept does too.

## 1. Format and constraints

Fill each line. The defaults below are the source method's values; change one only
with a reason written beside it.

- **Type:** silent, looping background video for the website. Primary placement:
  hero background, or a full-width section background behind text. Secondary
  placement: a square or vertical cut for social (see section 10).
- **Audio:** none. The file ships muted. Nothing in the edit may depend on sound.
- **Duration:** 32 to 40 seconds per loop. Six beats of 4 to 6 seconds plus two
  short transitions. The loop point must be invisible (see section 9).
- **Reading condition:** a visitor lands on any random second of the loop and may
  leave within five seconds. Every beat must, on its own, read as `<the craft
  idea>` and `<the client's subject>` at the same time. No beat is allowed to depend
  on the one before it.
- **Text:** no burned-in captions in the master. The site's HTML sits on top. One
  optional end-card variant exists for social use (section 8).
- **Pace:** slow. Every motion is a single continuous gesture, 60 to 80 percent of
  real-time speed in feel. No cuts within a beat, no camera whip, no zoom faster
  than a slow push.
- **Aspect:** master in 16:9 at 1920x1080 (or 3840x2160 if the pipeline supports it
  cleanly). Framing protects the **text-side third** for overlaid page text: the
  right third on a right-to-left page, the left third on a left-to-right page.
  Action lives in the centre and on the opposite side.

**Method: the timing logic.** Six beats of 4 to 6 seconds is not arbitrary.
Generated clips come out at 5 to 6 seconds, so one beat is one clip with room to
trim. Six beats is the length of a craft process told without rushing. The total
lands at 32 to 40 seconds, long enough that a returning visitor rarely sees the same
second twice. The reading condition is the hard constraint: it is why each beat
pairs one craft object with one subject object in a single frame, and why nothing
is explained across a cut.

**Method: the page's loop length.** The kit's `asset-pipeline` treats loops over
30 seconds as a streaming job. Decide at the lock whether the page carries the full
loop or a shorter cut of it, and write the decision here.

## 2. The concept

Three short paragraphs.

1. **The fusion.** Who the figure is, as two things in one body: `<the craft
   role>` and `<the client's profession>`. They perform the `<N>` canonical gestures
   of the craft, in their traditional order, but the material on the table is
   `<the client's subject, as physical objects>`.
2. **Why no explanation is needed.** Name the two sets of objects and what each is
   universally read as. Craft tools read as "made for you, by hand, with care".
   The client's documents and objects read as the profession. One object from each
   set in the same frame, in the same hands, is the entire message. The video does
   not explain the metaphor; it is the metaphor.
3. **Why this metaphor is true for this client, not just pretty.** One paragraph
   that ties the metaphor to the through-line in the profile (section 2 of
   `01-brand-and-client-profile.md`). The test: the metaphor must claim exactly what
   the service does. If the paragraph could be pasted into another client's document
   unchanged, the concept is not ready.

**The thesis line** (used on the social end card, and as the headline the site can
carry over the video):

- In the site's language: `<one line>`
- Alternative, taken from the client's own text: `<a verbatim line from the profile>`
- A second-language version only if the current brand already uses one.

> **Example.** Fusion: a bookbinder and a contracts lawyer in the same body. She
> folds, pierces, sews, presses and covers, but the sheets on her bench are the
> pages of an agreement. Truth test: the practice's work is making separate terms
> hold together under strain, which is what a binding does. Thesis line (English
> stand-in): "Agreements bound to hold."

## 3. The metaphor system

Each craft stage is paired with one object from the client's world and one meaning.
**The pairing is fixed. It is the grammar of the film and is not improvised against
later.**

| # | Craft stage | Gesture | Client object on the table | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `<stage>` | `<one physical gesture>` | `<object>` | `<meaning, in a few words>` |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |
| 4 | | | | |
| 5 | | | | |
| 6 | | | | |

**Method.**

- Use the craft's real stages in their real order. Someone who practises the craft
  should recognise the sequence. Invented stages read as fake to exactly the viewers
  who care most.
- The first stage is a human one (a consultation, a look, an intake). The last is a
  delivery. Between them, the stages move from understanding to deciding to
  finishing.
- One gesture per stage. If a stage needs two gestures, it is two stages or it is
  too busy.
- The client object is physical and recognisable in half a second: a plan, a
  stamped document, a bound report, a model. Never a screen.
- "What it says" is the claim the beat makes about the service. Write it as the
  client's customer would say it.

> **Example.**
>
> | # | Craft stage | Gesture | Client object | What it says |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | Receiving the work | Looking up from the bench, meeting the eye | Loose draft pages beside a closed portfolio | "I am listening" |
> | 2 | Folding and gathering | A bone folder creasing a sheet | The pages of the draft agreement | The facts put in order |
> | 3 | Marking and piercing | An awl piercing along a marked fold | A clause page with pencil marks in the margin | Marking what matters here |
> | 4 | Sewing | Needle and thread drawn through the sections | The gathered sections of the agreement | Tying the terms so nothing comes loose |
> | 5 | Pressing | The press screw turned down, a square held to the spine | The sewn agreement in a wooden press | Tested under pressure before it leaves |
> | 6 | Covering and handover | Cloth cover smoothed, ribbon laid, handed across | The bound agreement | Delivery; it holds |

## 4. The visual motif

Name the one shape from the logo that the profile marked as the most reusable motif
(a curve, a ribbon, a grid line, an angle). In the film the motif **exists
physically in every beat, as a different object each time**. List it per beat:

- Beat 1: `<object that takes the motif's shape>`
- Beat 2: `<...>`
- Beat 3: `<...>`
- Beat 4: `<...>`
- Beat 5: `<...>`
- Beat 6: `<...>`

**Method.** The viewer does not need to notice this consciously. It makes the film
feel like the brand without the logo appearing until the end card. Anyone who does
notice gets a small reward. Where the video sits under the logo in the page header,
the motif in the live video can line up with the motif in the logo above it. Choose
objects that take the shape naturally (something that coils, drapes, is drawn or is
cut); a shape forced onto a rigid object looks like a mistake.

> **Example.** Motif: the logo's single open arc. Beat 1 a coil of linen thread
> lying in the arc; beat 2 the lifted edge of a folding sheet; beat 3 a paper
> template strip curling off the bench; beat 4 the loop of thread between two
> stitches; beat 5 the ribbon marker hanging from the pressed block; beat 6 the
> ribbon marker laid across the cover.

## 5. The character bible

**Who.** `<the person>`, as a stylised avatar of themselves. Not a photoreal
deepfake; a consistent, recognisable figure. Or: nobody identifiable, hands and
silhouette only. State which, and attach the person's written agreement to a
generated likeness before anything else is written in this section.

**Physical description (fixed block, reused verbatim in all generation prompts).**

> `<one paragraph, copied from the profile's "the person, physically" paragraph:
> age range, complexion, hair, facial hair, glasses, eyes, expression, build>`

**Wardrobe (fixed).** `<garments, colours, what shows at the wrist, what is absent:
watch, rings>`. Note any prop that is worn in some beats only, and in which beats.
Say which existing photograph of the person the wardrobe matches.

**Face visibility rule.**

- Face sharp and visible: `<the first beat (looks up to camera) and the last beat
  (looks up on handover)>`.
- Face obscured in all other beats: turned down toward the work, in soft focus,
  cropped at the shoulders, or seen from three-quarter behind. **Hands are the
  protagonists.**

**Method.** This rule exists because identity drift across generated clips is the
single largest quality risk. Two sharp-face moments are enough for recognition;
everything else is hands, cuffs and the outline of the head in silhouette. A film
with no likeness at all drops the two sharp-face moments and loses very little.

**Hands.** `<description: build, grooming, jewellery (none), manner>`. Hands are in
every beat and must match across beats (skin tone, hair on the back of the hand,
nail shape). A hand reference sheet is generated alongside the face sheet.

**Behaviour.** Unhurried, deliberate, a craftsperson's economy of motion. Never
rushes, never fumbles, never looks at the camera except in the face-visible beats.

## 6. Setting, light, palette

**Setting.** One room that is both `<the craft's workroom>` and `<the client's
office>`. List the fixed furniture: the work surface and its material, what stands
behind it, the window and which way it faces, one or two set pieces in the
background, one object that is present but switched off or closed. No computers on
the table. Nothing that dates the room. **The room never changes between beats.**

**Light.** `<quality and direction: for example cold soft daylight from a window at
frame left, warm fill from the work surface>`. Low contrast, long soft shadows. No
flash, no neon, no rim light.

**Palette.**

- Dominant: `<two or three desaturated brand colours plus the material colours of
  the room>`
- `<brand dark>` for shadows and the main garment
- `<shadow tint>` as the shadow tint in the grade
- **One accent per beat** in `<the brand's accent colour>`, on a different object
  each time. List the object per beat. Only one object per frame carries the accent.
- Banned colours: `<every hue outside the palette, named>`.

**Method.** The accent rule does the same job as the motif: it brands every frame
without a logo, and it gives the grade something to protect while everything else
is pulled down. Naming banned colours matters because the classic versions of craft
tools often come in exactly those colours; write the on-palette substitute beside
each one in section 11.

**Texture.** `<the materials the camera should feel: paper, cloth, wood, metal,
dust>`. Shallow depth of field, 50 to 85 mm equivalent.

## 7. The script, beat by beat

Timings are targets for the edit. Generated clips should be 5 to 6 seconds each to
allow trimming.

Write every beat with the same eight fields, in this order:

### Beat `<n>`: `<title in the site's language>` (`<English title>`), `<start>` to `<end>`

- **Shot:** size, height and angle first; then what is in frame, in the order the
  eye finds it; then where the hands enter from and what each hand is doing.
- **Motion:** the one movement of the beat, with its extent ("the head rising about
  20 degrees", "two slow bites, the second longer than the first"). State what does
  not move.
- **Camera:** static, or one named slow move.
- **Face:** visible and sharp / not in frame / turned down, soft / three-quarter
  from behind.
- **Client object:** the object from the metaphor table. Any lettering on it is
  optional and is better left blank; generated lettering is unreliable and text
  belongs to the page.
- **Motif:** where the brand shape appears in this beat.
- **Accent:** the one object that carries the accent colour.
- **Meaning:** the "what it says" line from the metaphor table.

**Shot-design vocabulary.** Keep to this short list so the film has one visual
grammar and the prompts stay comparable:

| Term | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Medium close-up, eye level, slight low angle | The face-visible beats: the opening look and the handover |
| Top-down, or 45-degree high angle on the work surface | Hands working on a flat document; no face |
| Close-up, slightly above the surface | A single tool making a single mark; the lower edge of the face may sit soft at the top of frame |
| Low angle along the surface, very shallow depth of field | The decisive gesture (the cut, the pierce); the figure soft behind, three-quarter from the back |
| Medium shot | A set piece and the figure together; face turned down, soft |
| Static camera | The default for every beat |
| Slow push-in of about 2 percent | The opening look only |
| Very slow lateral track following the tool | One marking beat at most |
| Slow quarter-orbit | One beat at most, around a standing set piece |

**Beat pattern for a six-stage film.** The source method arranged its beats like
this; it is a good default.

1. **The meeting.** Face visible. The figure bent over the surface, hands resting on
   a closed object, the motif object beside it. One movement only: the head rises,
   the eyes meet the lens, a calm half-smile. No hand motion. Hold.
2. **The first working gesture.** High angle, hands only, entering from the side
   opposite the text zone. One slow continuous pull or stroke along a line of the
   client object.
3. **The mark.** Close-up. A tool draws or marks one clean line. Dust or a trace
   lifts. The lower edge of the face may appear soft at the top of frame.
4. **The decisive gesture.** Low angle along the surface. Two slow bites or one slow
   pass. The parted or changed material takes the motif's shape.
5. **The fitting.** Medium shot. A set piece carries the client object as if it were
   the craft's work in progress. Hands adjust it. Face turned down, soft, catching
   window light.
6. **The finish and the handover.** Two shots. **Shot A** (about 4 seconds): a
   close-up of the last finishing gesture on the client's final document. **Shot B**
   (about 4 seconds): medium shot, eye level; the figure completes the object,
   extends it toward the lens with both hands, looks up, holds a calm half-smile.
   Each action a single beat: finish, close, extend, look up.

### Loop return, `<start>` to `<end>` (about one second)

Dissolve from the held look of the last beat back into the held look of beat 1.

**Method: the loop logic.** The loop is invisible because the first and last frames
are the same framing, the same face and the same expression. The only differences
are the object under the hands (the finished piece against the unopened one) and
any prop worn in the early beats. Design the two frames together, as a pair, before
writing the beats between them. A loop that is fixed in the edit is always visible.

## 8. End card (social variant only)

Not in the website master. For the social cut, after the last beat's final shot
holds, cut to:

- A plain background in `<brand light or white>`. The logo's motif animates in as a
  single stroke in the reading direction of the brand (about 0.8 s). The name and
  descriptor fade in beneath it.
- One line below, set in the site's signature script style or its light text face:
  **`<the thesis line>`**
- Then, smaller: `<the one contact channel, taken from the profile's section 9>`
- Hold 3 s.

**Method.** The end card is built in the edit or in code from the real logo file
and real type. It is never generated. Text, logos and numbers from a video model
are unreliable and are paid for on every retry.

## 9. Loop and edit rules

- Cut on gesture completion, never mid-gesture.
- Transitions between beats: hard cut by default. One cross-dissolve allowed, at the
  loop point (last beat to beat 1).
- Speed: conform every clip to a consistent perceived speed. Most generated clips
  need a 0.8 to 0.9 times slowdown with optical-flow interpolation.
- Grade: unify all clips to one LUT. Lift shadows toward `<shadow tint>`, protect
  skin, pull saturation down 20 to 30 percent everywhere except the accent object.
- Grain: a fine uniform grain over the whole master hides generation artefacts and
  unifies textures.
- Safe area: keep hands and objects in the two-thirds away from the text side; the
  text-side third is reserved for overlaid page text.

## 10. Deliverables

1. **Web master, 16:9**, 1920x1080, 24 or 25 fps, H.264 MP4 (CRF about 20) and VP9
   WebM, muted, 32 to 40 s, seamless loop, under 8 MB if possible. A 3840x2160
   master if the pipeline allows.
2. **Poster frame** (first frame of beat 1) for the `poster` attribute and for
   no-autoplay fallbacks.
3. **Social square 1:1**, 1080x1080, with end card, 40 to 45 s.
4. **Social vertical 9:16**, 1080x1920, with end card, 40 to 45 s. Reframe each
   beat; most beats are top-down or close, so they survive the crop.
5. **The individual beat clips** as separate files, for use as section backgrounds
   or short inline loops on inner pages. Write the pairing: which beat sits behind
   which service page.
6. **Still frames** from each beat, graded, as a set of brand images for the site
   and social.

**Method.** Deliverables 5 and 6 are why the reading condition pays twice. A beat
that stands alone in the loop also stands alone as a section background and as a
still. Keeping the beat clips as separate files also leaves the door open to
re-cutting the film later as a scroll-scrubbed sequence.

## 11. What to avoid

Write the list for this client. The source method's list had these kinds of entry;
keep each kind and fill in the specifics:

- No voiceover, no dependence on music, no captions in the master.
- **No literal product of the craft.** The craft is the metaphor; showing its real
  finished product turns the film into an advertisement for the craft.
- No off-palette versions of the craft's tools. Name each tool whose classic version
  is off-palette and give the on-palette substitute.
- No clichés of the client's profession: `<list them: screens, calculators,
  banknotes, handshakes, whatever the category's stock imagery is>`.
- No face in motion-heavy beats; the identity will drift.
- No fast cuts, no transitions with wipes or glows.
- No stock establishing shots. The room is the whole world of the film.

## Check before the pitch

- Every beat passes the reading condition on its own: craft and subject both
  readable in a single frame, without sound, without the beat before it.
- The "why this is true" paragraph could not be pasted into another client's
  document.
- The metaphor table uses the craft's real stages in their real order.
- The motif and the accent each have one named object per beat.
- The first and last frames are designed as a matching pair.
- The likeness question is answered in writing, either way.

## The pitch

One concept, pitched once, in chat: the working title, the thesis line, the
three-paragraph concept, the metaphor table, the motif list, the character and room
in two lines each, and the cost estimate from the pipeline document. No generated
images accompany it. The Art Director's written lock is recorded in the project
before section 7 is written in full and before any generation runs.
