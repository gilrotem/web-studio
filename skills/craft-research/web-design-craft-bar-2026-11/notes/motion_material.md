## Key question 1 — Which motion patterns define the top tier in Aug–Nov 2026, which are tired, and what does the choreography look like in concrete terms?

### Takeaway
The top tier in mid-to-late 2026 is one continuous WebGL/Three.js scene driven by scroll (camera depth, room-to-room, narrative beats) with GSAP as the choreographer, gated behind a deliberate "unlock" gesture or hold-to-proceed, with text handled as masked line reveals; the Awwwards developer jury scores "Animations/Transitions" as a named sub-criterion and it was the highest sub-score on every 2026 winner checked (8.2–8.4/10). The scroll-triggered fade-in, parallax, and generic "AI-default" easing are the patterns practitioners now call tired.

**Concrete choreography values documented in current tooling**
- GSAP's canonical masked line reveal (docs for v3.15): `type: "words,lines"`, `mask: "lines"`, `autoSplit: true`, lines start at `yPercent: 100` and `opacity: 0`, `duration: 0.6`, `stagger: 0.1`, `ease: "expo.out"`, created inside `onSplit()` and returned so the tween survives re-splits. — [GSAP SplitText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/)
- GSAP 3.15 (13 Apr 2026) added `easeReverse` ("adaptive directional easing"): a separate easing curve for backward playback, recalculated from the point the direction changed, cascadable via timeline `defaults`; aimed at toggleable UI (menus, drawers, modals, tooltips) and interrupted animations; `yoyoEase` deprecated. — [GSAP 3.15 release](https://gsap.com/blog/3-15/)

**Patterns now regarded as tired**
- Scroll-triggered fade-in "scroll reveal" is called one of the most ubiquitous and most user-hostile patterns; guidance: do not use scroll-driven animation for primary content reveal; if a button needs to be there, it is there from the start. — [datadaily: scroll-fade antipattern](https://datadaily.pages.dev/scroll-fade-ux-antipattern); [dev.to: CSS scroll-driven patterns I ship in 2026](https://dev.to/raxxostudios/css-scroll-driven-animations-6-patterns-i-ship-in-2026-4bm6)
- NN/g's Katie Sherwin: subtle parallax still carries the usability problems of the flashier versions; it is pre-attentive and users may tune it out (banner blindness). — [Fast Company on NN/g](https://www.fastcompany.com/90309395/why-parallax-scrolling-needs-to-die). Academic findings: Wang & Sundar (2018, n=295) found parallax works through perceived coolness/vividness, not content; a Purdue thesis found parallax "more fun and professional" but no significant UX improvement. — [Memphis](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/9564); [Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI1544322)
- A Feb 2026 guide limits parallax to campaign microsites and desktop-first audiences and flags vestibular harm. — [Lovable guide (Feb 2026)](https://lovable.dev/en/guides/scrolling-designs-patterns-when-to-use)
- Practitioner commentary: default easing reads as generic; a fixed, intentional easing curve is what makes reveals feel designed. — [dev.to: motion patterns that stop looking generated](https://dev.to/kenimo49/framer-motion-view-transitions-api-4-ui-motion-patterns-that-stop-looking-ai-generated-25gg)
- A mid-2026 agency review says bento layouts and dark mode keep winning while 3D/WebGL and AI personalisation underdelivered for ordinary business sites. — [StudioMeyer (2026)](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)
- Custom cursors: Eric Bailey argues a low-vision user who needs a large high-contrast cursor can lose it entirely when a site replaces it — "a hard stop"; another critic ties cursor hijacking to the wave of "vibe-coded" sites. Awwwards still showcases custom cursor and custom preloader components. — [Eric Bailey](https://ericwbailey.design/writing/dont-use-custom-css-mouse-cursors); [Feedbagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/dont-hijack-my-mouse-pointer); [Awwwards inspiration: custom mouse](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/custom-mouse-stellare-agency)

### Inferences
- The Awwwards developer jury's sub-criteria in 2026 are, from the public score pages: Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design, Markup/Metadata. Animations/Transitions was the top sub-score on all three 2026 winners checked (8.2–8.4), and Accessibility the lowest on two (6.6). ## Key question 2 — Page transitions in 2026: cross-document View Transitions in production, Astro ClientRouter, Barba/Taxi, and what the best transitions look like

### Takeaway
Cross-document View Transitions are production-ready in Chromium (126+) and Safari (18.2+) but still not in stable Firefox as of the sources found; Google Search ships them for AI Mode switches, Interop 2026 added cross-document transitions as a focus area, and the argument of the moment is that a few lines of `@view-transition` CSS replaces ~180 kB of Barba+GSAP+Lenis router plumbing. Barba.js is still current (Codrops Astro tutorial, Apr 2026); Taxi.js produced no 2026 results.

### Cited Findings
- Same-document view transitions reached Baseline Newly Available with Firefox 144 (stable 14 Oct 2025); Firefox's initial version lacked view transition types. Chrome 137 added `view-transition-name: match-element` (same-document only); Chrome 140 added nested view transition groups (not in Firefox/Safari at the time), inherited animation longhands, and an async `finished` cleanup fix; scoped transitions (`element.startViewTransition()`) behind a flag from Chrome 140 with badges for Chrome 147; `document.activeViewTransition` in Chrome 142; React `<ViewTransition>` moved to `react@canary`. Post dated 8 Oct 2025. — [Chrome: View transitions 2025 update](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025); [web.dev, 16 Oct 2025](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)
- Cross-document support listed by Google as Chrome 126, Edge 126, Safari 18.2, Firefox unsupported. — [Chrome: Google AI Mode case study (28 Aug 2025)](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/google-ai-mode-view-transitions-case-study)
- Firefox status is contested across sources: web-features explorer shows Firefox for Android unsupported with a Firefox bug blocked since Dec 2024; a 2026 CSS-Tricks-derived article says Firefox support is "in progress"; one 2026 blog claims all three major browsers support both modes by May 2026 while another says cross-document remains behind a flag in Firefox as of April 2026. **Firefox cross-document status: unverified.** — [web-features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/cross-document-view-transitions); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions); [codercops 2026](https://blog.codercops.com/blog/css-view-transitions-scroll-animations-2026); [Vivian Voss 2026](https://vivianvoss.net/blog/view-transitions)
- Interop 2026 (announced 12 Feb 2026) carries View Transitions over from 2025 and expands the area to include cross-document view transitions; same-document interoperability stood at 90.8%. — [WebKit: Announcing Interop 2026](https://webkit.org/?p=17818)
- Google Search production case: cross-document transitions only for switching between standard Search and AI Mode, enabled by toggling `@view-transition { navigation: auto|none }` based on the clicked target; `<link rel="expect" blocking="render">` on elements near the top of the DOM with a watchdog timer that removes it on slow devices; auto-generated animations dropped frames on older devices so keyframes were rewritten with matrix math to run on the compositor; `view-transition-name` changed stacking so z-index was adjusted on the pseudo-elements; view transition types used to avoid naming collisions; unsupported browsers simply skip the animation. No durations, easings or reduced-motion handling given. — [Chrome: Google AI Mode case study](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/google-ai-mode-view-transitions-case-study)
- Astro's docs do not call `<ClientRouter />` deprecated but say its extra features "will increasingly become unnecessary" as browser APIs evolve; `fallback` options `animate` (default), `swap`, `none`; it includes a media query that disables all view-transition animations including fallbacks under `prefers-reduced-motion`; built-in directives `fade` (default crossfade), `slide` (out left, in from right, reversed on back), `initial`, `none`; example `fade({ duration: "0.4s" })`; latest major referenced is Astro 7. — [Astro docs: View transitions](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/)
- A 25 Aug 2026 practitioner post: Barba.js + GSAP + Lenis + cleanup adds about 180 kB of libraries and ~750 lines of code, less than a third of which is animation — the rest re-initialises Webflow, restarts Lenis, refreshes ScrollTrigger; the native approach keeps browser back/forward, scroll restoration, analytics pageviews, focus and screen-reader announcements. Pattern: a persistent "splash" element with a `view-transition-name` and an animated `clip-path` using `shape()` whose control point bows outward, old page drifts up, new page slides in; reduced motion via `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { @view-transition { navigation: none; } }`; Firefox gets a normal page load. Inspiration: brandcreatives.nl (Webflow + Barba + GSAP + Lenis). — [usecue: Page transitions done right (25 Aug 2026)](https://www.usecue.com/blog/page-transitions-done-right/)
- Barba.js is ~7 kB, History-API based, and still current — Codrops published "Creating Custom Page Transitions in Astro with Barba.js and GSAP" on 8 Apr 2026. Swup is named as the lighter alternative (single blog claim). — [Barba GitHub](https://github.com/barbajs/barba); [Codrops 8 Apr 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/08/creating-custom-page-transitions-in-astro-with-barba-js-and-gsap/); [swup mention](https://svilenkovic.com/3d/swup-page-transitions)
- Chrome's docs on cross-document transitions for MPAs. — [Chrome docs: cross-document](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/cross-document)

### Inferences
- For a multi-page professional-services site in late 2026, native cross-document View Transitions are the defensible default: progressive enhancement, zero router code, correct focus/scroll/analytics behaviour, and a one-line reduced-motion opt-out. Barba/Taxi remain justified only when a persistent WebGL canvas or audio must survive navigation.
- "What the best transitions look like" in 2026, from the examples: one persistent element (logo, splash, hero image) carried across pages with a shaped clip reveal, while the old page drifts and the new one slides; short, compositor-only, and skipped entirely when unsupported or when motion is reduced. The Google case study shows even default-generated keyframes can drop frames on older phones and must be checked.
- RTL note (inference): Astro's `slide` is defined as out-left/in-from-right; in a Hebrew site the "forward" direction convention is reversed, so a slide transition should be mirrored (out-right/in-from-left) or replaced with a direction-neutral fade/clip. No source addresses RTL page transitions.

## Key question 3 — Text choreography: SplitText practice, masked line reveals, variable-font axis animation, accessibility of split text

### Takeaway
The 2026 reference pattern is SplitText 3.13+ (free) with `mask: "lines"`, `autoSplit` and `onSplit`, lines rising 100% from under a clip over ~0.6 s with expo.out and a 0.1 s stagger; but a February 2026 screen-reader audit by Adrian Roselli showed SplitText's default `aria-label`/`aria-hidden` scheme fails in JAWS/Chrome, Narrator/Edge and VoiceOver/Safari, and his advice is to stop splitting words into letters at all.

### Cited Findings
- SplitText (GSAP v3.15 docs; features marked require 3.13+): `mask` ("lines" | "words" | "chars", one at a time) wraps each unit in a clipping element; `autoSplit` (default false) reverts and re-splits on font load or container-width change; `onSplit()` runs after each split and, if it returns a tween/timeline, SplitText reverts the old one and carries progress over; `aria` default "auto" adds `aria-label` to the split element and `aria-hidden` to child units, "hidden" hides the whole thing, "none" adds nothing (for when you ship a separate screen-reader copy); `propIndex` adds `--word: n` style CSS variables; `deepSlice` (default true) subdivides nested `<strong>`/`<a>` across lines; `smartWrap`; `ignore`. Reference reveal: `document.fonts.ready` then `SplitText.create(".split", { type: "words,lines", mask: "lines", autoSplit: true, onSplit: self => gsap.from(self.lines, { duration: 0.6, yPercent: 100, opacity: 0, stagger: 0.1, ease: "expo.out" }) })`. — [GSAP SplitText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/)
- SplitText rewrite shipped in 3.13 ("50% smaller, 14 new features"); it can be used without loading GSAP core; it is free for everyone thanks to Webflow. — [GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/); [Webflow: SplitText rewrite](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-splittext-rewrite)
- GSAP's a11y guidance: since 3.13.0 SplitText adds `aria-label` on the parent and `aria-hidden` on children by default to stop letter-by-letter reading; this drops nested links, so set `aria: false` and add a screen-reader-only duplicate while keeping the animated copy hidden; duplicating many DOM nodes hurts performance so use sparingly. — [GSAP a11y resource](https://www.gsap.com/resources/a11y/)
- Adrian Roselli (posted 5 Feb 2026, updated 1 Apr 2026): each letter/word/line gets a wrapper `<div>` with `aria-hidden`; `aria-label` on a `<div>` (generic role) is prohibited, GSAP's example avoids it by using headings but the docs do not state the risks (unreliable translation, WCAG 2.5.3 Label in Name on controls). Results: NVDA/Firefox works; JAWS/Chrome never announced; Narrator/Edge only first letter; VoiceOver macOS/Safari only a subset of letters, read letter by letter; Orca/Firefox works; TalkBack/Chrome works; TalkBack/Firefox and VoiceOver iPadOS fail. GSAP's linked CSS-Tricks hiding article is out of date. Recommendation: "find another method"; avoid SplitText until GSAP issue #642 is addressed. The sr-only-duplicate / revert-after-animating advice comes from GSAP's Cassie Evans in the comments, not from Roselli. — [Adrian Roselli, Feb 2026](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/02/you-know-what-just-dont-split-words-into-letters.html)
- Motion's split-text component uses the same scheme: `aria-label` with the unsplit text on the host and `aria-hidden="true"` on child spans. Griffo sets `aria-label` only on elements that may be named (headings, landmarks) and injects an sr-only copy elsewhere. — [Motion docs: text animation](https://motion.dev/docs/text-animation); [Griffo accessibility guide](https://www.mintlify.com/dimicx/griffo/guides/accessibility)
- Variable-font axis animation practice: CSS transition on `font-variation-settings` (e.g. `'wght' 400` to `700` on hover); `animation-timeline: scroll()` paired with `font-variation-settings` for scroll-driven weight; every axis that should stay constant must appear in both keyframes or interpolation breaks; per-character staggered weight waves are CPU-heavy on mobile, so limit to key headings; Framer components fall back to scroll-based modes on touch because phones cannot hover. None of these sources is 2026-dated. — [camoa guide](https://camoa.github.io/dev-guides/css/css-craft/variable-font-animation/); [ics.media](https://ics.media/en/entry/201008/); [Google Fonts / Edge demo](https://googlefonts.github.io/microsoft-edge-variable-fonts-demo); [Framer Font Weight Hover](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/MmYGz11bx5oYTJWv4yYS/)

### Inferences
- The defensible 2026 pattern for a professional site: split by **lines only**, mask the line, animate the line box (not characters), wait for `document.fonts.ready`, use `autoSplit` for resize, and revert the split after the intro so the DOM returns to plain text. Character-level splitting is now an accessibility liability with documented screen-reader failures.
- Set `aria: "none"` and keep the real heading text in the DOM as the accessible copy (visually hidden) rather than relying on `aria-label` on a heading; this follows Roselli's objection and GSAP's own "duplicate" advice.
- RTL notes (inference, no source addresses Hebrew splitting): SplitText line detection relies on measured positions, so `autoSplit` after `document.fonts.ready` is more important for Hebrew webfonts that load late; a `yPercent` line rise is direction-neutral and safe; an `xPercent` slide-in should be mirrored (positive x enters from the right in RTL reading order); character splitting of Hebrew is doubly risky because of final-letter forms and nikud combining marks, which `type: "chars"` would separate — avoid it. Variable-font weight animation for Hebrew requires a Hebrew variable font that exposes `wght`; most of the cited demos use Latin fonts (Inter, Roboto Flex).

- No 2026 source on splitting Hebrew or any RTL text with SplitText or Motion.
- No measured data on how masked line reveals affect LCP when the hero heading is the LCP element.
- No GSAP response to Roselli's issue #642 found by Nov 2026.

## Key question 4 — Scroll-telling: pinned scenes, scrub, image-sequence scrubbing, horizontal tracks, CSS scroll-driven animations vs GSAP ScrollTrigger, Lenis adoption and criticism

### Takeaway
CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline`) became cross-browser with Safari 26.0 (15 Sep 2025) and are an Interop 2026 focus, and Chrome 146 (10 Mar 2026) added time-based scroll-triggered animations (`animation-trigger`, Chromium only); GSAP ScrollTrigger remains the tool for pinning, scrub with inertia and WebGL sync. Lenis 1.3.x (lerp 0.1, duration 1.2 s) now disables smoothing by default under reduced motion, but Thoughtworks and accessibility practitioners still flag smooth-scroll focus and input inconsistencies.

### Cited Findings
- Safari 26.0 (15 Sep 2025) added Scroll-driven Animations; Chrome/Edge have had them since 115 (Jul 2023); Firefox behind a flag as of early 2026 — wrap in `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`. Safari 26.5 fixed scroll-driven animations not pausing when `animation-play-state` was set to paused; a third-party article says 26.4 added threaded (off-main-thread) scroll-driven animations **(unverified against Apple notes)**. — [WebKit: Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/); [WebKit guide to scroll-driven animations](https://www.webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/); [WebKit: Safari 26.5](https://webkit.org/?p=17938); [buildmvpfast 2026](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/css-scroll-driven-animations-replace-js-2026)
- Interop 2026 lists Scroll-driven Animations and Scroll Snap as focus areas. — [WebKit: Interop 2026](https://webkit.org/?p=17818)
- `animation-trigger` (scroll-triggered, time-based: starts when an element crosses a threshold and then runs for its own duration, unlike scroll-linked `animation-timeline` which maps 0–100% to scroll and reverses) uses `timeline-trigger` with `view()` and ranges like `entry 100% exit 0%` (= `contain`) and an optional active range (`/ cover`); actions named `play-forwards play-backwards`; `trigger-scope` limits trigger names. - ScrollTrigger `containerAnimation` (fake horizontal scroll) requires `ease: "none"` on the container tween and does not support pinning or snapping on the container-based triggers. — [GSAP ScrollTrigger docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- Image sequences vs video: OPTIKKA (Codrops, 16 Oct 2025) moved from HTML5 video to frame sequences for control and performance, loading a device-specific sequence path per breakpoint; scrubbing `<video>` is unreliable (async seeking, keyframe stutter, mobile Safari autoplay/inline restrictions); decoding is the main cost; 200 frames at 2000 px is 400 MB+ uncompressed; serve e.g. 60 frames on mobile vs 120 on desktop; or load a single fallback image instead of the sequence. — [Codrops OPTIKKA (16 Oct 2025)](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/16/creating-smooth-scroll-synchronized-animation-for-optikka-from-html5-video-to-frame-sequences/); [dev.to scroll-scrubbed canvas](https://dev.to/pratham7711/how-i-made-a-scroll-scrubbed-video-portfolio-fast-nextjs-15-gsap-canvas-1mj6); [CSS-Tricks image sequence](https://css-tricks.com/?p=308477)
- Lenis (lenis.dev, formerly lenis.darkroom.engineering): under 5 kB, "no accessibility trade-offs" (vendor claim), keeps native scrollbar, no CSS transforms, `lenis/snap` for snapping, horizontal/infinite/element scrolling; powers Locomotive Scroll. - Criticism: Thoughtworks Technology Radar (Oct 2024) says Lenis can have accessibility issues with vertical and horizontal interactions that could confuse users with disabilities; Lenis smooths only wheel and touch, not scrollbar drag or keyboard, so behaviour differs by input; Equalize Digital's Amber Hinds: failing to move keyboard focus with smooth scroll is a widespread mistake. No 2026 formal audit of Lenis found. — [Thoughtworks radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis); [GSAP forum on Lenis vs ScrollSmoother](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/36030-use-scrollsmoother-features-with-lenis-scroll/); [Admin Bar: watch out for smooth scroll](https://theadminbar.com/accessibility-weekly/watch-out-for-smooth-scroll)

- Practitioner guidance for reduced-motion users lists parallax, horizontal scroll hijacking and zoom-on-scroll as patterns to disable. — [dev.to raxxo 2026](https://dev.to/raxxostudios/css-scroll-driven-animations-6-patterns-i-ship-in-2026-4bm6)
- Chrome DevTools: CPU throttling does not touch the GPU process; expensive paint/composite, box-shadows and blur filters can look fine on desktop and run far slower on a phone. — [Chrome: DevTools grounded in real-world data (4 Apr 2025)](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/devtools-grounded-real-world)

**RTL notes for horizontal tracks**

### Inferences
- Division of labour in 2026: CSS `animation-timeline` for linked progress effects (progress bars, parallax-lite, reveal-on-view with `@supports` guard), `animation-trigger` only as a Chromium enhancement, GSAP ScrollTrigger for pinned scenes, scrub with `scrub: 0.5–1` smoothing, WebGL camera sync and anything needing snapping or horizontal pinning.

## Key question 6 — Motion specification: how studios write motion specs in 2026 (tokens, choreography tables, storyboards, Rive/Jitter/Figma) and how they verify motion

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- Atlassian: reach for semantic motion tokens first; tokens bundle duration, easing curve and motion properties; scale from 100 ms (pressed states, quick exits) to 150 ms (interactive highlights). ## Key question 7 — Reduced motion as design: how top sites design the prefers-reduced-motion version, what WCAG 2.2 and the Awwwards developer review expect, examples that do it well

### Takeaway
The 2026 consensus is "reduce, don't remove": design two motion states, keep functional motion (progress, feedback) as a gentle crossfade, remove decorative loops, parallax and large swipes, and offer an in-page toggle; WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires pause/stop/hide for auto-playing motion over 5 seconds and 2.3.3 (AAA) requires interaction-triggered motion to be disableable, and prefers-reduced-motion alone does not satisfy 2.2.2 for autoplaying video. Awwwards exposes an "Accessibility" developer sub-score, on which 2026 winners scored lowest (6.6–7.0).

### Cited Findings
- GSAP's guidance: use `gsap.matchMedia()` with `(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` for full motion and `reduce` for simplified; decide per animation — is it triggering (flashing, hard colour cuts, large x/y swipes) and is it decorative or functional; remove decorative, keep functional but simplify (Apple's example replaces a swipe with a gentle crossfade); offer a user toggle because OS settings are not known to everyone. — [GSAP a11y](https://www.gsap.com/resources/a11y/)
- Lenis: `respectReducedMotion` true by default; smoothing off and programmatic scrolls jump; preference changes apply live. — [Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- Astro ClientRouter disables all view-transition animations including fallbacks under reduced motion; native pattern is `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { @view-transition { navigation: none; } }`. — [Astro docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/); [usecue, Aug 2026](https://www.usecue.com/blog/page-transitions-done-right/)
- web.dev: `reduce` means minimise movement, "preferably to the point where all non-essential movement is removed"; decorative animated gradients, parallax and background videos are most likely to bother users; its demo removes reveal animations and leaves only regular scrolling. — [web.dev: prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)
- Webflow (GSAP's owner): design two motion states, full and reduced, where the reduced state is "an alternative design that communicates the same transitions, hierarchy changes, and state updates through different means"; reduced motion should be part of the design specification, not a development afterthought. — [Webflow: motion and accessibility](https://webflow.com/blog/motion-and-accessibility)
- Smashing Magazine: reduce when the value is `reduce`; do not treat `no-preference` as an informed opt-in; prefers-reduced-motion helps with 2.3.3 but autoplaying/looping content still needs explicit pause controls under 2.2.2. — [Smashing: designing with reduced motion](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2020/09/design-reduced-motion-sensitivities)
- WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A): auto-starting moving/blinking/scrolling information lasting more than five seconds and presented in parallel with other content needs a pause/stop/hide mechanism. 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA): motion triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential. Hidde de Vries argues honouring prefers-reduced-motion is acceptable for 2.2.2 conformance "with exceptions". — [W3C Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide); [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions); [hidde.blog](https://hidde.blog/meeting-2-22-pause-stop-hide-with-prefers-reduced-motion/)
- Awwwards developer sub-scores on 2026 winners: Accessibility 6.60 (Why Zero), 7.00 (USAvionix), 6.60 (Hubtown) — the lowest or near-lowest sub-score in each case. — [Why Zero](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/why-zero); [USAvionix](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/usavionix); [Hubtown](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/hubtown)
- Awwwards' inspiration library has a "Reduced Motion Fallback" entry (Silverfish Studios), showing the pattern is catalogued as a design element. — [Awwwards inspiration: reduced motion fallback](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/reduced-motion-fallback-silverfish-studios)
- Greenspector found Awwwards usability scores stayed high even for a nominee with ~40 accessibility errors and ~30 contrast errors (Wave), and argued objective accessibility measures should enter the evaluation. — [Greenspector (2022)](https://greenspector.com/en/resources/blog/analysis_sites_nominated_mobile_excellence_awwwards/)
- A figure of roughly 25–30% of visitors with reduce enabled circulates from one studio's analytics; anecdotal. — [monotonomo journal](https://monotonomo1-project.pages.dev/journal/prefers-reduced-motion-premium-patterns/)

### Inferences
- The reduced-motion version that reads as designed in 2026: same layout and hierarchy, opacity crossfades (150–250 ms) instead of travel, masked text appears in place without the rise, hero video replaced by its poster, WebGL scene rendered as a still or slowly breathing frame with no scroll-camera, page transitions off, Lenis smoothing off, and a visible toggle in the header or footer that also lets full-motion users opt out. This synthesises GSAP, Webflow, web.dev and Lenis guidance; no single source lists it.
- The Awwwards developer review does score accessibility, but winners pass at ~6.6–7.0 while scoring 8+ on motion; reduced-motion quality is therefore not what wins the Developer Award, but a professional-services site in Israel has stronger reasons (IS 5568 / WCAG conformance) to design it fully.

### Inferences
- "60 fps at 4x on 390 px" is a reasonable house gate but Chrome's own guidance has moved to calibrated presets plus field (CrUX p75) alignment; a stricter 2026 gate is: no frame over 16.7 ms in the FPS track during scroll and interaction at the mid-tier preset, zero LoAF entries over 50 ms attributable to animation scripts, and a real mid-range Android pass because GPU-bound effects (blur, shaders, shadows) are invisible to CPU throttling.
- LCP with a hero scene: the Google case study and the poster-fallback practice imply the hero's LCP candidate should be a static poster or heading painted before the WebGL/sequence initialises, with the scene fading in over it; no 2026 source measures LCP for a WebGL hero specifically.
- Award-level JS budgets "actually hit" in 2026 are not public; the WPO sub-scores (7.2–8.0) on winners suggest they pass a jury's impression of speed rather than a numeric threshold. For a services site, the 200 kB JS / Lighthouse 90+ practitioner budget is a stricter and more defensible target than anything Awwwards publishes.

### Gaps
- Awwwards Developer Guidelines document (current performance/accessibility checklist) is not public; its 2026 contents are unknown.
- No dataset of Lighthouse scores, JS bytes or INP for 2026 Awwwards winners (a Habr study from Jan 2025 exists but only its comments page was indexed).
- No 2026 measurement of LCP for sites with WebGL or image-sequence heroes.
