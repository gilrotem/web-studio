# Hebrew typography, RTL web craft and Israeli web standards — state as of October–November 2026

Scope: premium Hebrew-only, RTL, WordPress site for a sole-practitioner licensed professional in Israel (the worked example of a regulated profession throughout is a real-estate appraiser, שמאי מקרקעין). Research date: 9 November 2026. Items marked **(background)** predate 2025; items marked **(unverified)** could not be confirmed against a primary source.

Method note: web search plus direct fetches of primary pages (W3C i18n, Google Search Central, Kol Zchut, SII, Awwwards, foundry sites), plus raw-HTML downloads of drukerlaw.co.il and five top-tier Israeli law-firm sites to read fonts, builders and scripts off the actual markup. The Fontsource API (`api.fontsource.org/v1/fonts?subsets=hebrew`) was queried on 9 Nov 2026 as a proxy for the Google Fonts Hebrew catalogue.

## Key question 2 — Hebrew type craft (size vs Latin, line height, measure, weight contrast, numerals, mixing, variable axes, OpenType, text-wrap)

### Takeaway
Hebrew has effectively no ascenders (only lamed) and few descenders (final letters), so a Hebrew face at the same nominal size reads smaller and denser than Latin; practice compensates with slightly larger size and more leading than Latin body text. Variable axes now available for Hebrew include weight, width, optical size, slant and "round"; numerals in Hebrew text are Western digits and should be height-matched to the Hebrew body. `text-wrap: balance` is safe everywhere (Chrome 114+, Firefox 121+, Safari 17.5+); `text-wrap: pretty` is Chrome 117+/Safari 26+, Firefox contested — use as progressive enhancement; Hebrew-specific behaviour of these properties has no published documentation.

### Cited Findings
- Hebrew has one ascender — lamed — "traditionally at least as tall as an entire mem height, but in modern typography it's usually shortened to save space" — [baba: Hebrew Fonts compared](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts)
- In the Ubuntu font Hebrew design (Dalton Maag), a Hebrew glyph at full Latin cap height was judged too big; the team set Hebrew body height at 90% of cap height; x-height-level Hebrew was "too small to stand up against the Latin" — **(background, 2010)** [Canonical blog: initial Hebrew trials](https://canonical.com/blog/intial-hebrew-trials)
- Dinamo's Favorit Hebrew (Hebrew by Fontef) ships an axis letting users "scale the letters up to match the Latin uppercase height if desired" — [ABC Dinamo: Favorit Hebrew](https://abcdinamo.com/typefaces/favorit-hebrew)
- A Hebrew-LaTeX paper notes Hebrew letters should be slightly taller than Latin for equal apparent size, so a nominal 10pt Hebrew looks too small next to 10pt Latin — **(background)** [Sivan Toledo, Typesetting Hebrew with LaTeX](https://www.tau.ac.il/~stoledo/Pubs/eutupon.pdf); a word-processor forum user sizes Hebrew 115–125% of the English — **(anecdotal)** [Mellel forum](https://forum.mellel.com/viewtopic.php?t=1821)
- Myriad Hebrew's designer settled on making Latin and Hebrew figures equal height after testing, because Latin figures in multilingual fonts are too tall for Hebrew and Hebrew-height figures look too short in Latin — **(background, 2013)** [Typekit: Myriad Hebrew specimen](https://blog.typekit.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/myriad-hebrew-online-specimen.pdf)
- A vendor tool suggests Hebrew body line-height 1.6–2.0 and headlines 1.1–1.3 — **(vendor rule of thumb)** [fontygen Hebrew font generator](https://fontygen.com/font-generator/hebrew/); a Hebrew-language hosting blog recommends 1.5–2× the font size — [Hostragons (HE)](https://www.hostragons.com/he/blog/אופטימיזציה-של-טיפוגרפיה-לאתרים-איך-לשפר-קריאות-וחווית-משתמש/)
- Measure: web.dev's Hebrew-language guide gives 45–75 characters per line as appropriate and 66 as ideal, and warns that large line-height on long lines makes the eye's return sweep harder — [web.dev Learn Design: Typography (HE)](https://web.dev/learn/design/typography?hl=he)
- Pairing guidance: pair faces that share weight and x-height, and specify a fallback because a browser lacking Hebrew glyphs silently substitutes a system font; Heebo is cited as drawn with matched Hebrew and Latin — [baba: Hebrew Fonts compared](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts)
- Hebrew type designers interviewed by Makor Rishon: in running text the reader should flow and not stop on letters; the face must hold up at small and large sizes — **(background)** [Makor Rishon / nrg](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/1/ART2/897/165.html)
- Variable axes actually shipping for Hebrew (Fontef): weight + optical size (Frank Rühl Variable), width + round (Haim Variable), weight + width (Aharoni Variable), weight + monospace (Miriam Variable), slant (Cupidon) — [fontef.com](https://fontef.com/); width axis in Noto Sans Hebrew — [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-hebrew/about)
- `text-wrap: balance`: Chrome 114+, Edge 114+, Firefox 121+, Safari 17.5+ — [savvy.co.il: text-wrap pretty/balance](https://savvy.co.il/en/blog/css/css-text-wrap-pretty-balance/); `text-wrap: pretty`: Chrome 117+, Edge 117+, Safari 26+ per one source; Firefox support contested between "not yet (early 2026)" and "Firefox 134" — [CSS Portal: text-wrap-style](https://www.cssportal.com/css-properties/text-wrap-style.php); [modern-css.com: text-wrap-style](https://modern-css.com/reference/properties/text-wrap-style/); WebKit shipped `pretty` in Safari Technology Preview April 2025 — [mjtsai.com, 11 Apr 2025](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/04/11/css-text-wrap-pretty-in-webkit); unsupported browsers ignore the property and fall back to normal wrapping — [Chrome Developers: text-wrap balance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-text-wrap-balance?hl=pl)
- W3C: when Latin text sits at the start of an RTL title it can be misplaced; inserting `&rlm;` fixes it; lines with no strong characters (e.g., "123-456") render LTR — [W3C i18n: Structural markup and RTL text](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir)

### Inferences
- Practical 2026 defaults for Hebrew body copy: 17–19px equivalent (one step above a Latin default), line-height ~1.6–1.7 (Hebrew needs more than Latin's 1.5 because the square letters fill the body with no ascender/descender rhythm), measure ~55–65 Hebrew characters, and a text face with a sturdy medium/regular rather than light weights (Hebrew's heavy horizontals thin out badly at light weights on screen). These are inferences from the glyph-structure facts above, not published numbers.
- Weight contrast between display and text should be made with one family's weight axis or two faces of similar body height; avoid Latin-ascender-driven faces whose Hebrew companion was shrunk to x-height.
- Numerals/Latin inside Hebrew: prefer a Hebrew face whose digits were drawn to Hebrew height (Fontef and AlefAlefAlef faces do this), or isolate Latin runs with `<bdi>`/`unicode-bidi: isolate` and nudge with `font-size: 0.95em` via `:lang(en)` — the Myriad Hebrew note is the evidence that height matching is the issue to solve.
- OpenType: kerning (`kern`) and contextual alternates matter; nikud not needed for a professional-services site. `text-wrap: balance` on Hebrew headlines works (it is script-agnostic), `pretty` is safe as enhancement; no Hebrew-specific bug reports were found.

### Gaps
- No authoritative Hebrew-specific web-typography guideline (W3C, foundry or Israeli design body) publishing numeric size/line-height ratios was found; all numbers above are vendor rules of thumb or single-project case studies.
- No documentation of `text-wrap: balance/pretty` behaviour specifically with Hebrew or RTL text.
- Meir Sadan's "An Introduction to Hebrew Type" (Medium) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
- No source on which OpenType features (`calt`, `ss01`…) Hebrew webfonts from Israeli foundries actually implement.

## Key question 3 — RTL implementation in 2026 (logical properties, :dir(), bidi isolation, icon mirroring, GSAP horizontal, forms, WordPress rtl.css, browser bugs)

### Takeaway
The 2026 consensus is: `dir="rtl"` on `<html>` in markup (never via CSS), logical properties everywhere, `:dir(rtl)` only for the few physical cases, `dir="auto"`/`<bdi>` for inserted or user text and form fields, and `dirname` for forms. WordPress core still ships `rtl.css` and WPML still documents it, but WordPress.org's own 2024 theme work moved to logical properties; GSAP ScrollTrigger has no native RTL mode and horizontal-scroll pins must be reversed by hand. Even a premium Israeli law-firm site built by Layer (drukerlaw.co.il) still ships per-breakpoint `*.rtl.css` files.

### Cited Findings
- W3C: add `dir="rtl"` to the `html` tag whenever overall direction is RTL; do not rely on `lang` for direction; do not apply base direction with CSS "because direction information should be part of the markup so it survives when CSS is unavailable"; use logical `start`/`end` values; avoid HTML attributes with `left`/`right`; `dir="auto"` picks direction from the first strong character and suits forms and runtime-inserted text; browsers skip `<bdi>` content when detecting direction; on `<input>` `dir="auto"` right-aligns Hebrew and left-aligns English; on `<textarea>` `dir="auto"` sets direction per paragraph; `dirname` submits the computed direction with the form — [W3C i18n: Structural markup and right-to-left text in HTML](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir)
- Mozilla's Firefox RTL guidelines: prefer logical properties; fall back to `:dir(rtl)` only when no RTL-aware property exists or left/right is intended; logical properties can be wrong for LTR text embedded in RTL contexts, where `text-align: match-parent` is often needed — [Firefox source docs: RTL Guidelines](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/code-quality/coding-style/rtl_guidelines.html)
- Logical properties mapping (width→inline-size, left/right→start/end) described in 2026-dated guides — [DEV: Logical properties of CSS layout](https://dev.to/nickbenksim/logical-properties-of-css-layout-for-different-languages-2pca); [John Kavanagh: direction-aware layouts](https://johnkavanagh.co.uk/articles/using-css-logical-properties-for-direction-aware-layouts/); Israeli agency Savvy's explainer — [savvy.co.il: CSS logical properties](https://savvy.co.il/en/blog/css/css-logical-properties/)
- Properties with no logical equivalent (float, position offsets in some cases) must be scoped per direction or avoided — [Acquia Cohesion docs: RTL styling](https://cohesiondocs.acquia.com/6.2/user-guide/rtl-languages)
- WordPress: WPML's guide still instructs "create a new rtl.css file and put it in your theme's root directory" — [WPML: Build RTL WordPress themes](https://wpml.org/documentation/theme-compatibility/build-rtl-wordpress-themes/); a December 2024 WordPress.org changeset re-implemented the Breathe theme's RTL overrides with logical properties — [meta.trac.wordpress.org changeset 14308](https://meta.trac.wordpress.org/changeset/14308); core ticket on logical properties — [core.trac.wordpress.org #54322](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/54322)
- Evidence from the field: drukerlaw.co.il (built 2024–2026 by Layer) loads `main.rtl.css`, `front-page.rtl.css` and `*-above-768/992/1200/1600.rtl.css` — i.e., a compiled rtl.css pipeline, not logical properties — [drukerlaw.co.il raw HTML](https://www.drukerlaw.co.il/)
- GSAP: no official RTL mode for ScrollTrigger; the forum fix for reversed horizontal pins is `flex-direction: row-reverse` (or `direction: rtl`) and tweening from the end value to 0 — [GSAP forum: horizontal scroll but reversed](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/40221-horizontal-scroll-but-it-is-reversed/); a request for native RTL support in ScrollTrigger got no official answer in the thread — [GSAP forum: right-to-left horizontal scroll support](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/28089-right-to-left-horizontal-scroll-support/); `dir="rtl"` on a scroll container breaks ScrollToPlugin centring math — [GSAP forum, RTL tag](https://gsap.com/community/tags/rtl/)
- Lingo.dev's 2026 Next.js RTL guide repeats the same core recipe (`dir` on root, logical properties, icon mirroring) — [lingo.dev: RTL in Next.js](https://lingo.dev/en/nextjs-pages-router-i18n/right-to-left-languages)

### Inferences
- For a new WordPress theme in 2026, write logical properties in a single stylesheet and do not generate `rtl.css`; keep `:dir(rtl)`/`[dir=rtl]` scoped overrides only for floats, `transform: scaleX(-1)` on directional icons (arrows, "back", progress), shadows and background-position. Register the theme as RTL-aware so WordPress does not try to load a missing `rtl.css`.
- Phone numbers, addresses with house numbers, Latin brand names and email addresses inside Hebrew copy should be wrapped in `<bdi>` or `<span dir="ltr">`; contact-form inputs for phone/email should carry `dir="ltr"` while free-text fields use `dir="auto"` — follows directly from the W3C guidance.
- For horizontal scroll-telling with GSAP in RTL, the safest pattern is a vertically scrubbed section whose panels are laid out with `direction: rtl` and translated with positive x values, not `scrollLeft` (which is negative/zero-based inconsistently across engines) — inference from the forum threads.

### Gaps
- No 2026 list of outstanding browser RTL bugs (Chrome/Safari/Firefox issue trackers were not searched); the known quirk reported is `scrollLeft` sign behaviour in RTL containers (hinted in GSAP threads; not independently verified here).
- No WordPress-core document declaring logical properties the official replacement for `rtl.css`; the ticket/changeset show direction of travel only.
- Browser support for `:dir()` was not checked on caniuse in this pass (it is widely reported as supported in all evergreen browsers since 2024 — **unverified here**).

## Key question 4 — Israeli web-accessibility law (IS 5568, Regulation 35, WCAG version, exemptions, statement, enforcement, overlays)

### Takeaway
The duty comes from the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law via the Service Accessibility Regulations 2013 (Regulation 35 and 35ו), implemented through IS 5568 Part 1 at Level AA; the standard remains legally anchored to WCAG 2.0 AA (a 2023 edition tracks WCAG 2.1 more closely; no WCAG 2.2 adoption found). An accessibility statement is mandatory for every covered site, even an exempt one. Per Kol Zchut (updated Aug 2026), a sole trader registered as עוסק פטור is fully exempt from web adjustments, and turnover ≤ ₪120,000 gives a renewable 3-year exemption — but commercial blogs still quote ₪100,000, so the number is contested. Enforcement is by civil suits (up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage), Commission accessibility orders and fines; no official Israeli position on overlay widgets was found, though the market and litigation trend treat them as insufficient.

### Cited Findings

**Legal basis and standard**
- Law: חוק שוויון זכויות לאנשים עם מוגבלות; Regulations: תקנות שוויון זכויות לאנשים עם מוגבלות (התאמות נגישות לשירות), תשע"ג-2013; Standard: IS 5568 Part 1 (websites), adopting W3C WCAG; IS 5568 Part 2 covers digital documents — [Kol Zchut: web and app accessibility (updated 1 Nov 2026)](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות)
- The ISOC-hosted SII document for Part 1 (2021 edition) references WCAG 2.0 — [SII 5568 PDF via ISOC](https://www.isoc.org.il/files/docs/5568.pdf); ISOC explainer on the regulations (new vs existing site cut-off 26/27 Oct 2017) — [ISOC: all about the accessibility regulations](https://www.isoc.org.il/freedom-of-internet/accessibility/all-about-accessibility)
- SII page for IS 5568 Part 2: published 28/09/2023, Reshumot notice 22/10/2023, no amendment sheets — [SII standard page id=6753](https://www.sii.org.il/he/lobby/standardization/standard-page?id=6753); a government-hosted PDF of SI 5568-2 (September 2023) — [gov.il: SI 5568-2 Sept 2023](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/israeli_accessibility_standards_pdf/he/sitedocs_si-5568-2-september-2023.pdf)
- Required level is AA, subject to exemptions — [Accessible.org.il: IS 5568 vs WCAG 2.1](https://accessible.org.il/תקן-נגישות-הישראלי-והבין-לאומי-5568-ו-wcag-2-1/); several vendors state WCAG 2.1 AA is best practice beyond the legal minimum and that IS 5568 "including the September 2023 edition" remains legally anchored to WCAG 2.0 AA — [user-a.co.il: Israeli standards vs WCAG](https://user-a.co.il/en/standards/israeli-accessibility-standards-vs-wcag); contradicted by vendors claiming "IS 5568:2020 aligns with WCAG 2.1" — [accessiBe: IS 5568](https://accessibe.com/compliance/is-5568), [clym.io](https://clym.io/accesibility-regulations/israeli-standard-is-5568)
- No source found announcing adoption of WCAG 2.2 into IS 5568 or the regulations as of Nov 2026 — [search summary; tabnav explainer](https://tabnav.com/he/info-center/accessibility-standard-5568-israel-law); [digitale.co.il 2026 guide](https://digitale.co.il/he/חוק-הנגישות-לאתרים-בישראל-2026/)

**Who is covered**
- Sites providing a public service or a Second-Schedule service; duty falls on the operator/holder, not the builder; for-profit private entities, public authorities and NGOs; sites serving the Israeli public even if hosted abroad — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות)

**Exemptions (Regulation 35ו) — Kol Zchut, updated 26 Aug 2026**
- Heavy economic burden, automatic when annual turnover ≤ ₪1,000,000: עוסק פטור — full exemption from web accessibility; average annual turnover < ₪120,000 — 3-year exemption, renewable while the average stays ≤ ₪120,000; ₪120,000–₪1,000,000 — 3-year exemption only for existing sites launched before 26.10.2017 (new sites must be accessible), with accessible contact details published; above ₪1,000,000 — application to the Commission required; not available to public authorities — [Kol Zchut: exemptions](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פטור_מחובת_הנגשה_לאתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות)
- Technological infeasibility: not automatic; granted per adjustment by a licensed service-accessibility expert for 3 years, renewable once; beyond 6 years needs the Commission; exempt body must provide alternatives, disclose the exemption in the accessibility statement, and show the approval on request — [Kol Zchut: exemptions](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פטור_מחובת_הנגשה_לאתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות)
- Low-user site: automatic if registration is required and ≤ 500 registered users — same source.
- Contradicting commercial sources quote a ₪100,000 threshold ("תקנה 35ו(ז): עוסק פטור או מחזור שנתי ממוצע עד 100,000 ₪"), disagree on whether the exemption is automatic or requires application, and one older source cites ₪300,000/₪1,000,000 tiers — [user-a.co.il: who must comply](https://user-a.co.il/בעלי-עסקים/אילו-אתרי-אינטרנט-חייבים-בהנגשה-ומי-פטור-מחובה-זו); [vee.co.il: exemption](https://vee.co.il/exemption-from-website-accessibility/); [ecom-isr.com guide](https://www.ecom-isr.com/3989/accessibility/website-accessibility-israel-guide/); [web-a.co.il](https://www.web-a.co.il/blog/חוק-הנגישות-באינטרנט/)
- Every source agrees: exemption from adjustments does **not** exempt from publishing an accessibility statement — [vee.co.il](https://vee.co.il/exemption-from-website-accessibility/); [user-a.co.il](https://user-a.co.il/חקיקה/פטור-מהנגשת-האתר-ידעתם-שאפשר-לקבל-כזה)
- Video accessibility duty applies only above ₪5M average turnover and to public authorities — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות)

**Accessibility statement contents**
- Must cover: how the site was made accessible; how to operate the accessible site; services not made accessible and alternatives; how to contact the operator about accessibility problems — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות); vendor guidance adds: accessibility level, known deficiencies, reachable from every page — [ritarget.co.il](https://www.ritarget.co.il/articles/hok-negishut-atarim-israel-2026)

**Enforcement**
- 60-day cure notice before action; criminal fine ≈ ₪150,000; civil compensation up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage; Commission accessibility orders with ₪7,500/day fines (double for corporations); class actions possible; standing for the person, the Commission, or disability NGOs — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות)
- Vendor-quoted administrative fines: up to ₪36,500 (individual) / ₪73,000 (corporation) — **(unverified, marketing source)** [ritarget.co.il](https://www.ritarget.co.il/articles/hok-negishut-atarim-israel-2026)
- District-court ruling (reported Jan 2024) limiting class certification where the only violation is a missing accessibility statement — [Firon law firm](https://www.firon.co.il/?p=15487); [Barnea (barlaw.co.il)](https://barlaw.co.il/?p=46191)
- The wave of demand letters: not documented in any primary source found; vendors describe "many businesses sued and paying significant compensation" — **(unverified)** [equalweb.com](https://www.equalweb.com/p/34479/11529/israel_web_accessibility)
- Commission position paper on Regulation 37 (websites) defines an accessible site structurally (perceive, understand, operate, orient) — [kshalem.org.il PDF of Commission paper](https://www.kshalem.org.il/uploads/pdf/article_3109_1559059641.pdf)

**Overlay widgets**
- No published Commission statement on overlays was found. A vendor article states most overlay plugins "לא עומדים באמת בתקן 5568" and are a supplement, not a replacement — [ritarget.co.il](https://www.ritarget.co.il/articles/hok-negishut-atarim-israel-2026). The FTC's 2025 action against Israel-founded accessiBe is the best-documented regulatory item on overlays — [FTC complaint: accessiBe](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/complaint.222_3156_-_accessibe_final_consent_package_without_signatures.pdf)
- In practice, the premium Israeli sites sampled all load UserWay: drukerlaw.co.il (`cdn.userway.org/widget.js`), gornitzky.co.il, meitar.com, pearlcohen.com — [raw HTML, fetched 9 Nov 2026](https://www.drukerlaw.co.il/)

### Inferences
- A sole-practitioner licensed professional (for example a lawyer, an appraiser or an accountant) is a covered private service provider. If registered as עוסק פטור he is exempt from the technical adjustments per Kol Zchut, but an accessibility statement is still mandatory and the site should state the exemption basis and contact route. If he is עוסק מורשה with turnover above ₪120,000, a **new** site must meet IS 5568 (WCAG 2.0 AA minimum; build to 2.1/2.2 AA as practice). Verify the threshold against the current regulation text before relying on ₪120,000 vs ₪100,000.
- The "June 2026 deadline for small businesses" quoted by some agencies does not appear in Kol Zchut or the regulations and should be treated as marketing.
- Shipping a UserWay-type overlay is common at the top of the Israeli market but does not substitute for native conformance; it is defensible only as an add-on to a natively accessible build.
- Statement should name an accessibility contact (רכז נגישות duty formally applies to larger service providers; a sole practitioner names himself), the conformance level claimed, the date of last audit, known gaps, and the exemption basis if any.

### Gaps
- Full text of Regulation 35/35ו (Nevo) was not fetched; threshold conflict (₪100,000 vs ₪120,000) remains unresolved at the primary-source level.
- SII page for IS 5568 Part 1 (id=6751) was not fetched; the exact current edition date of Part 1 and whether it formally references WCAG 2.1 is unconfirmed.
- No Commission (נציבות) publication on overlay widgets or a 2025–2026 enforcement statistics report was found.
- Rachaz negishut (coordinator) threshold for web services specifically was not confirmed.

## Key question 5 — Israeli professional-services web conventions (contact, GBP, Waze, Bit/PayBox, .co.il, Hebrew SEO, structured data, privacy policy under Amendment 13)

### Takeaway
Israeli service sites lead with phone and WhatsApp (`wa.me/972…`), a short lead form, a Waze deep link (`https://waze.com/ul?ll=…&navigate=yes`) alongside Google Maps, and a Google Business Profile with identical NAP. Bit/PayBox are consumer P2P rails with business tiers, not web checkout — irrelevant for a professional-services practice beyond invoicing. Google is ~98% of Israeli search; Google's structured-data docs support `LocalBusiness` subtypes (use the most specific, e.g. `ProfessionalService`) with `name`, `address`, `geo`, `telephone`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `url`, `priceRange`. Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law (in force 14 Aug 2025) makes the section-11 notice at the point of a contact form a five-item duty and has already been enforced with a ₪12,000 fine on a small company for an incomplete online form.

### Inferences
- Minimum contact stack for a sole-practitioner professional-services site: sticky mobile bar with `tel:` + `wa.me/972…` (international format, no leading zero), a 3-field lead form (name, phone, the matter; for an appraiser, what to value) with the five-item s.11 micro-notice and privacy-policy link beside the submit button, office address with Waze + Google Maps links, and a GBP in the correct professional category (for an appraiser, "Real estate appraiser") with identical name/address/phone to the site.
- JSON-LD: one `ProfessionalService` (or `LocalBusiness` with `additionalType`) node with `name`, `address`, `geo`, `telephone`, `url`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `areaServed`, `knowsAbout`, plus `Person` for the licensed professional (for example a lawyer, an appraiser or an accountant) with licence number (`hasCredential`) — Google only promises rich results for the generic LocalBusiness fields, so the specific type is for clarity, not a guaranteed feature.
- Privacy policy content for a small professional-services site with a contact form: identity and contact of the controller, what the form collects and why, voluntary nature, recipients (hosting, email, CRM), retention, rights of access/correction, security measures, cookies/analytics. Avoid the "בעל מאגר" wording. No DPO or registration is needed for a sole practitioner's contact-form data (not in the four DPO categories; far below 10,000/100,000 thresholds).
- Bit/PayBox links belong on the invoice, not on the site; a "pay" page is not an Israeli convention for appraisers (the regulated-profession example researched).
- For a licensed professional (for example a lawyer, an appraiser or an accountant), prominently state the licence (for an appraiser: רישיון שמאי מקרקעין מס' …) and the regulated scope — in the appraiser example, the Chamber's action against unlicensed valuation sites makes this a trust and compliance signal.
