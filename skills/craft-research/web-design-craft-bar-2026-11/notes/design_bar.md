# Web design craft bar, October–November 2026: what award-tier sites look like and what juries reward

Research date: 9 November 2026. Everything below is sourced from pages fetched on that date unless marked "background". Awwwards site pages carry numeric jury scores but no written jury commentary, so "why it won" lines are inferred from the scores, tags and highlighted elements on each winner page and are marked as such.

Note on method: Awwwards winner pages do not list typefaces for any of the winners fetched (the "Fonts" field was empty on every page), so the typography findings lean on gallery curation (Siteinspire, Fonts In Use, Typewolf) and on jurors' writing rather than on winner metadata.

---

## Key question 1 — What have Awwwards SOTD / SOTM / Developer Award winners in Aug–Nov 2026 had in common?

### Takeaway
The Aug–Nov 2026 winner set is dominated by scroll-driven narrative sites built with GSAP (plus WebGL/Three.js when there is a story to carry), set in two-colour palettes (black/white or near-black plus one accent), with the Developer Award attached to almost every SOTD. Jury scores cluster tightly at 7.2–7.7 overall; the one outlier (Santioni Spirits, 8.09) is an illustrated, sound-designed WebGL story whose Animations/Transitions sub-score hit 9.40.

### Inferences
- The award bar in late 2026 is not "most 3D" but "one continuous, directed narrative"; WebGL is present where the story needs it (Why Zero, Santioni, Cartier, Spyker) and absent where typography or product clarity carries the site (Grids on WebGL but black/white; Heron on Canvas; LPAS on none).
- Platform is no longer disqualifying: Webflow sites won SOTM (ERA Residence) and SOTD (Heron, Moto), and a WordPress site (LPAS) took a Developer Award above its design score. The jury scores the result, not the stack.
- Two-colour palettes are now the house style of award-tier work; colour is spent on a single accent or on imagery, not on gradients or multi-hue systems.
- Accessibility is the systematic weak spot even among winners; a site that pairs strong transitions with an 8.0 accessibility score (Cartier) stands out on the developer axis.

## Key question 2 — What do Awwwards' published criteria and jury commentary say now? Any 2026 changes?

### Takeaway
The published system is unchanged in substance: Design 40 / Usability 30 / Creativity 20 / Content 10, at least 18 jurors with the three outlier scores dropped, HM at ≥6.5, Developer Award at >7 across six sub-areas (Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design, Markup/Meta-data). No 2026 criteria change was found; what has changed is the emphasis in juror writing — performance on mid-range phones, reduced-motion fallbacks and "continuous" transitions are now named as gating factors.

### Cited Findings
- Weights: Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%, introduced as "A fair and transparent system." The page does not define what each criterion measures. — [Awwwards: Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- Process: sites go to at least 18 jury members; "the 3 scores furthest from the average are removed automatically"; voting lasts 5 days; a site can win SOTD early with a high jury score and at least 10 PRO-user votes; only validated Professional users' votes count. — [Awwwards: Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- Thresholds: "HMs are awarded to sites that are scored 6.5 or more from the jury"; SOTD goes to the highest-scoring sites; the eight highest-scoring sites each month are SOTM nominees and re-reviewed, with user votes carrying "extra weight"; all SOTM winners are SOTY nominees, announced in February. Developer Award requires a score "higher than a 7", judged by a developer jury against external Guidelines (a linked Google Doc, not reproduced). — [Awwwards: Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- The page has no "last updated" date; it references "Jury 2026". — [Awwwards: Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- Developer Award sub-areas, as displayed on every 2026 winner page: Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design, Markup/Meta-data. — e.g. [Awwwards: Why Zero](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/why-zero); [Awwwards: Cartier](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cartier-le-choeur-des-pierres)
- FAQ: two separate votes (jury, users); HM needs jury ≥6.5 and user score at the same threshold; the jury score is visible only to SOTD winners. — [Awwwards FAQ via search](https://www.awwwards.com/faqs/)
- Jury recruitment 2026: applications closed 6 March 2026; Main Jury requires at least one SOTD; a Young Jury exists for applicants aged 25 or younger. — [Awwwards: Become a jury member in 2026](https://www.awwwards.com/become-an-awwwards-jury-member-in-2026.html)
- Juror reading of the axes (Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026): Design = "typographic system, layout and negative space, deliberate color and contrast, consistency across the whole site"; Usability = "navigation clarity, performance, responsiveness on real phones, accessibility"; Creativity = "originality with a clear purpose, judged on concept, interaction, and execution"; Content = "quality of copy, imagery, and video, and how well they fit the design." "Design and Usability together are 70% of your score." A separate Mobile Excellence track requires 70/100 against Google's mobile criteria. He lists no 2026 changes. — [Hon Tran: Awwwards Judging Criteria (2026)](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- Hon Tran's do/don't: carry one concept through every section; keep animation near 60fps ("a hero animation [that] drop[s] to around 40fps… reads as broken"); test on a mid-tier Android phone; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and show the final state; no placeholder copy or assets; "get the fundamentals right, then add one bold idea" — chasing creativity first "can produce an impressive site that scores around 6.2." — [Hon Tran: Awwwards Judging Criteria (2026)](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- Hon Tran (27 Jun 2026) on what juries reward: art direction ("a specific point of view in which every type, color, and grid choice serves one idea"), directed motion, performance on "real, mid-range devices", a reduced-motion fallback, and "continuous transitions… not hard cuts or plain fades." A gap in any one "typically tops out in the mid-7s." His juror checklist: check the static frame; throttle CPU 4× and network; watch the transitions; toggle reduced motion. — [Hon Tran: 10 Best Award-Winning Websites of 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Metabole Studio (31 Jul 2026): judges look for "a distinct perspective, controlled risk, and ideas they haven't seen before"; "Tidiness is the floor, not the achievement"; "Clean and correct" never wins; generic animation libraries read as "off-the-shelf"; decorative scrollytelling "wrapped around a page with nothing to say is seen through quickly"; a premium template "is shared by thousands of sites, so it cannot be singular." — [Metabole: What Awwwards Juries Reward](https://metabole.studio/en/blog/awwwards-winning-website)
- Managed Code (30 Jun 2026): "custom interaction design" is the differentiator; most submissions fail on Usability incl. Core Web Vitals; "over 15,000 sites submitted per year; fewer than 365 earn Site of the Day"; award-level sites "typically cost $30K to $200K and take 8 to 24 weeks." — [Managed Code](https://www.managed-code.com/blog-post/can-ai-design-award-winning-website-site-of-the-day-2026)
- Conflict to note: Hon Tran estimates winners "typically land in the mid-to-high 8s after combining jury and PRO votes", but every 2026 SOTD page fetched shows a jury overall of 7.24–8.09. The 8s likely refer to the blended jury+user figure, which the pages show separately as "Awwwards Users" (9.1–9.8). — [Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria) vs winner pages cited in Q1

### Inferences
- With Design+Usability at 70% and jurors explicitly testing on throttled mid-range phones, a site that is visually ambitious but drops frames or ignores reduced motion is capped in the mid-7s, which is where the bulk of 2026 SOTDs sit; the sub-8 cluster suggests most winners are trading some Usability for Creativity.
- The developer jury's six sub-areas make the Developer Award a quasi-mandatory companion to SOTD in 2026 (≈96% of SOTDs carried it), so "award-tier" now implies passing both juries.

### Gaps
- The Developer Award Guidelines document (Google Doc) was not retrievable; exact developer criteria text is unverified.
- No official Awwwards statement of a 2026 criteria or weight change was found; absence of evidence, not proof of none.
- No primary jury interview or Awwwards blog commentary from 2026 was found; juror writing here is from one juror's own blog (Hon Tran) plus agency blogs.

## Key question 4 — Which trends are rising in late 2026, and which are called tired or template-grade?

### Takeaway
Rising, on the evidence of what shipped and won: directed scroll narrative, two-colour palettes with one accent, typographic experiments (Obys; Typography Honors nominees), sound as part of the experience, and "the interface behaves like the product" for technical brands. Called tired by critics and jurors: kinetic type as a demo-reel effect, universal 3D heroes that cost 800kB–2MB and drop frames, heavy glassmorphism, blob/organic heroes, bento as a default, hard cuts/plain fades, and "clean and correct" template work. WebGPU has not yet produced a 2026 SOTD on the evidence found.

### Inferences
- The trend articles of January 2026 (kinetic type, 3D everywhere, glass) have been partly disowned by mid-year; what the juries actually rewarded Aug–Nov is narrower: restraint in palette, one directed narrative, transitions as the craft showpiece, and type/illustration doing the work that photoreal 3D did in 2023–24.
- "Template-grade" in late 2026 reads as: multi-hue gradients, glass cards, bento default, Spline hero, fade-in-on-scroll, no reduced-motion state, and no single idea that the type, grid and motion all serve.
