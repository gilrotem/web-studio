# Directed restraint sets the 2026 craft bar

Research date: 9 November 2026. Synthesised from five research notes (design bar, execution stack, AI design method, motion and material, Hebrew/RTL/Israel) gathered on that date. Where a claim is an inference from the evidence rather than something a source states, the text says so.

The award-tier bar in November 2026 is not spectacle but direction. The Awwwards Sites of the Day of August–November 2026 cluster at **jury scores of 7.2–7.7** (one outlier at 8.09), share **two-colour palettes**, GSAP-choreographed scroll narratives and a Developer Award on **roughly 90 of 94 winners**, and are judged by jurors who now throttle the CPU, test on mid-range Android phones and toggle reduced motion before scoring ([Awwwards SOTD](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_day/); [Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). Professional-services sites almost never reach SOTD; their realistic ceiling is Honorable Mention plus category honours, and in Israel the top of the law and finance market is conservative, English-first WordPress/WPML work with an accessibility overlay, which leaves a premium Hebrew-only professional-services site a wide-open gap to occupy. The execution stack has settled: **GSAP 3.15.1** free under Webflow, **Lenis 1.3.27**, **Vite 8.3.6**, **three.js r187**, **WordPress 7.1 "Mary Lou"** with a hybrid PHP + `theme.json` theme and script modules, and a CSS platform in which scroll-driven animations and same-document view transitions are now Baseline. The most professional method for agent-built design is a written design package before code, three distinct directions narrowed to one, deterministic lint plus measured checks plus a fresh-context critic, and a three-round cap before a human looks. Motion craft means masked line reveals, compositor-only transitions, a designed reduced-motion state and a 60 fps pass at calibrated mid-tier throttling, with character-level text splitting now a documented screen-reader liability. Hebrew adds its own gates: a sturdier text weight and more leading than Latin, logical properties instead of `rtl.css`, a Hebrew-only font subset preloaded, IS 5568 at WCAG 2.0 AA minimum with a mandatory accessibility statement, and Amendment 13's five-item notice beside every form. The studio should align to the developer-jury standard that Cartier set (8.0 accessibility alongside 8.6 transitions), not to the median winner.

## Juries reward one directed narrative, two colours and a build that passes both panels

The Awwwards winner set for 9 August to 9 November 2026 is unusually legible once the individual score pages are read together. Eleven 2026 winners fetched list **exactly two colours in ten cases**, and seven of those are black or near-black with white or off-white (Grids, FORMAT, A24, Spyker, Cartier, Moto, USAvionix); the accented ones spend colour once — Why Zero's green #01C654, Heron AI's orange #F15534, LPAS's warm tan #E3C1AA ([Awwwards: Why Zero](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/why-zero); [Awwwards: Heron AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/heron-ai); [Awwwards: LPAS](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lpas)). GSAP appears in the technology tags of every winner page fetched except Santioni Spirits (tagged only WebGL), and "Storytelling" is the dominant tag across Why Zero, ERA Residence, Santioni, Spyker and Heron AI ([Awwwards: Santioni Spirits](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/santioni-spirits); [Awwwards: ERA Residence](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/era-residence)). The highest jury score in the window, **Santioni Spirits at 8.09**, is an illustrated, sound-designed WebGL story whose Animations/Transitions sub-score reached **9.40** — illustration, not photoreal 3D, carried the top site ([Awwwards: Santioni Spirits](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/santioni-spirits)). Why Zero, Site of the Month for September, was built "scroll-driven and unlocked by drawing a zero" and, in the studio's own words, "tuned from the start to run smoothly on a budget phone, which the studio considers its most important achievement" ([BUNQ LABS](https://bunqlabs.com/insights/why-zero-awards)).

The scoring system itself has not changed: **Design 40 / Usability 30 / Creativity 20 / Content 10**, at least 18 jurors with the three outlier scores removed, Honorable Mention at 6.5, and a Developer Award for scores above 7 across Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design and Markup/Meta-data ([Awwwards: Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)). What has changed is the emphasis in juror practice. Juror Hon Tran's published checklist is to check the static frame, throttle CPU 4× and the network, watch the transitions and toggle reduced motion; he says a hero animation that drops "to around 40fps… reads as broken", that a gap in any one of art direction, directed motion, mid-range-device performance or a reduced-motion fallback "typically tops out in the mid-7s", and that chasing creativity before fundamentals "can produce an impressive site that scores around 6.2" ([Hon Tran: Judging Criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria); [Hon Tran: Best Award-Winning Websites](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). Metabole's reading of the jury is blunter: "Tidiness is the floor, not the achievement"; "clean and correct" never wins; generic animation libraries read as "off-the-shelf"; a premium template "is shared by thousands of sites, so it cannot be singular" ([Metabole](https://metabole.studio/en/blog/awwwards-winning-website)).

Two measured patterns matter most for a studio setting its own bar. First, the Developer Award has become a quasi-mandatory companion: roughly 90 of 94 SOTDs in the window carried it, and every 2026 Site of the Month does ([Awwwards SOTD p.1–3](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_day/); [Awwwards SOTM list](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_month/)). Second, **Accessibility is the systematic weak spot even among winners** — 6.60 on Why Zero and Santioni, 6.80 on Heron, Spyker and A24 — while Animations/Transitions is the top sub-score on every page (7.40–9.40). The exception is Cartier's "Le Chœur des Pierres" by makemepulse, where Accessibility scored **8.00** alongside Animations 8.60 and the developer score (7.84) beat the design score (7.33) ([Awwwards: Cartier](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cartier-le-choeur-des-pierres)). That profile — motion craft and accessibility passing together — is the one a professional-services site can realistically own, because the law in its market demands the second half anyway.

Platform is no longer disqualifying. ERA Residence won Site of the Month for August on Webflow; Heron AI and Moto Finance are Webflow SOTDs; and **LPAS, a California architecture practice on WordPress + GSAP**, took a Developer Award (7.54) above its design score (7.19) with a restrained portfolio, a full-page video menu and a footer "continuous flow" ([Awwwards: LPAS](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lpas); [Awwwards: ERA Residence](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/era-residence)). The jury scores the result, not the stack. What it marks down is equally consistent across critics: "polished templates with no point of view", effects "bolted on", "hard cuts and fades between states", 3D heroes that drop frames on mid-range Android, bloated pages ("9MB taking six seconds to paint") and ignored reduced motion ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). StudioMeyer's six-month retrospective adds the field evidence: kinetic typography became "the demo reel", a single Spline hero "adds 800kB to 2MB of JavaScript", heavy glassmorphism "caused 15–30% FPS drops on mid-tier Android", and bento grids are "now the default layout" and therefore no longer differentiating ([StudioMeyer](https://dev.to/studiomeyer_io/web-design-trends-2026-what-actually-held-up-after-six-months-23p8)). WebGPU, despite the marketing, produced no SOTD in the window; the only WebGPU-labelled Awwwards result in 2026 is a January Honorable Mention ([Awwwards: 3D WebGPU ArchiViz](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/3d-webgpu-archiviz)).

## Professional-services sites top out at Honorable Mention, and Israel's bar is lower still

Award juries rarely crown law, finance or consulting work. Awwwards' finance tag in 2026 shows **one SOTD (Moto Finance, a fintech card on Webflow, 7.3)** and a run of Honorable Mentions; wealth-management entries reached only nominee or HM level; no law-firm SOTD was found in 2025–2026 ([Awwwards finance search](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/finance/); [Awwwards: Moto Finance](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/moto-finance)). Moto's developer sub-scores are all 7.0–7.4 — even rather than peaky — and the site borrows luxury codes (near-black #0B0C07, video hero, "a premium financial card for modern wealth") rather than banking codes. The critic-curated top tier of large law firms reads the same way: Kirkland & Ellis with a "monochromatic core" where "the design recedes so the substance leads"; Gibson Dunn "mostly black and white, with one or two accents, described as 'quiet luxury'", results leading the homepage; Sidley Austin with "elegant serif or refined sans-serif headlines", deep navy and generous white space ([Block Agency](https://blockagency.co/blog/law-firm-website-designs/)). The deliberate rule-breakers (Crosby's dark-mode startup register, Bick Law's wildlife imagery animating from monotone to colour) prove the same point from the other side: a single bespoke idea is what lifts a services site out of the template class ([Azuro Digital](https://azurodigital.com/law-firm-website-examples/)). Junca Studio's deep-tech survey supplies the content rules that travel: every claim carries a unit, hardware is shown as a real photograph when it exists, the buyer is named early, and "the page should be usable before any intro animation finishes" ([Junca Studio](https://juncastudio.com/news/the-12-best-deeptech-websites-in-2026)).

Israel's award-level work is studio and portfolio work, not professional services. On the Awwwards Israel listing the 2026 SOTDs are **Realevate (27 Sep 2026, jury 7.22; Accessibility 6.60)** and CoffeeTech (16 Jul 2026), both credited to Or Halevi with MAKETHECUT; the Honorable Mentions are Wix Studio showcases and studio sites, and none is a Hebrew professional-services site ([Awwwards: Israel](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/Israel/); [Awwwards: Realevate](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/realevate)). The actual local bar for law and finance is set by Layer (Hod Hasharon, a Moburst company) whose portfolio includes Gornitzky, Meitar and Pearl Cohen; the raw HTML of those sites shows `lang="en-US"` defaults, WordPress + WPML, Swiper carousels and a UserWay overlay, mostly without a motion library ([layer.co.il](https://layer.co.il/); [gornitzky.co.il](https://gornitzky.co.il/); [meitar.com](https://meitar.com/)). drukerlaw.co.il, the closest Hebrew-first example read, is a Layer child theme that self-hosts **Ploni Light and Medium** (AlefAlefAlef) with Miriam Libre from Google Fonts, uses one teal-blue (#1E5A73) on white, ships per-breakpoint `*.rtl.css` files, loads UserWay, and has an English hero headline on a Hebrew page and two different phone numbers in CTA and footer ([drukerlaw.co.il](https://www.drukerlaw.co.il/)). Its build hygiene is good (WOFF2 self-hosting, `display=swap`, correct `dir`/`lang`, statement in the footer); its design language is a corporate template with two weights and no typographic hierarchy beyond them. The inference is direct: a Hebrew-only professional-services site that pairs a variable Hebrew display face with real editorial hierarchy, native accessibility and restrained GSAP or CSS scroll-driven motion would sit above the entire local professional-services tier and closer to the Awwwards-Israel studio level, where Hebrew RTL work is almost absent.

The galleries confirm who the studio would be judged against. Siteinspire's largest categories are Agencies & Consultancies (2,407) and Typographic (2,105); it has no Law, Finance or Consulting tags at all; Minimal Gallery's top tags are Portfolio, Personal and Agency ([Siteinspire](https://www.siteinspire.com/); [Minimal Gallery](https://minimal.gallery/)). A services firm that adopts the quiet typographic register is therefore compared with studio sites, not with other firms. The type direction visible across Fonts In Use and Typewolf — a high-contrast or old-style serif for display (Adobe Caslon, Cardinal, Baskerville, Signifier) paired with a neutral grotesk for UI (Inter, Neue Haas Grotesk, Styrene, Switzer) — is the pattern to translate into Hebrew, not to import as Latin ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/); [Typewolf](https://www.typewolf.com/site-of-the-day)). Godly itself now redirects to recent.design, a broader gallery mixing web, motion, branding and app screens, which suggests the 2026 curation lens treats a website as one artefact in a brand's motion and identity system ([recent.design](http://recent.design/?ref=godly)).

## The execution stack has settled, and WordPress delivery means a hybrid theme on script modules

The motion layer is stable and free. GSAP's npm `latest` is **3.15.1, tagged 4 November 2026**, under a "Standard 'no charge' license"; 3.15.0 (13 April 2026) introduced `easeReverse` and deprecated `yoyoEase` with full backward compatibility, and no breaking changes appear in either 2026 release ([npm gsap](https://registry.npmjs.org/gsap/latest); [GSAP 3.15 blog](https://gsap.com/blog/3-15/); [GSAP tags](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP/tags)). Every formerly paid plugin — SplitText, ScrollSmoother, MorphSVG — is included because "Webflow's support" funds the library ([gsap.com/pricing](https://gsap.com/pricing/)). The 3.15.1 patch matters directly for Hebrew: **SplitText now honours line breaks in right-to-left text**, fixes Safari line-height on masked elements via `vertical-align: top`, and notes that `specialChars` may not be fully honoured because of `Intl.Segmenter` pre-splitting ([GSAP 3.15.1 tag](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP/releases/tag/3.15.1)). Lenis is at **1.3.27 (15 October)**, with `respectReducedMotion: true` as the default since 1.3.26, and its documented GSAP bridge is three lines — `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`, `gsap.ticker.add(t => lenis.raf(t * 1000))`, `gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0)` — with `autoRaf` left `false` ([Lenis releases](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/releases); [Lenis README 1.3.27](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/darkroomengineering/lenis@1.3.27/README.md)). Lenis's own limitations are worth weighing against a services site: no CSS scroll-snap, a 60 fps cap on Safari and 30 fps in Low Power Mode, and smoothing that applies to wheel and touch but not scrollbar drag or keyboard, which Thoughtworks flagged as a source of confusion for users with disabilities ([Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md); [Thoughtworks radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis)). The choice is Lenis (window-native, lighter) or ScrollSmoother (transform wrapper, `data-speed` effects), never both; and for a page without a WebGL layer to keep in sync, native scroll with CSS scroll-driven effects avoids the input inconsistency entirely.

The CSS platform crossed several lines in the last twelve months. **Scroll-driven animations became Baseline Newly available on 27 October 2026** with Firefox 159; same-document view transitions have been Newly available since 14 October 2025; `@starting-style`, popover, `text-wrap: balance` and relative colour are Newly available; `:has()`, `color-mix()` and container queries are Widely available ([webstatus.dev scroll-driven-animations](https://api.webstatus.dev/v1/features/scroll-driven-animations); [webstatus.dev query](https://api.webstatus.dev/v1/features?q=id%3Aview-transitions+OR+id%3Apopover+OR+id%3Acontainer-queries+OR+id%3Ahas+OR+id%3Acolor-mix+OR+id%3Arelative-color+OR+id%3Aoklch+OR+id%3Atext-wrap-balance+OR+id%3Awebgpu+OR+id%3Aspeculation-rules+OR+id%3Ascroll-state-queries+OR+id%3Aif); [web.dev October 2026](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)). Still Limited: cross-document view transitions (Chrome 126, Safari 18.2, no Firefox), `text-wrap: pretty` (no Firefox), `if()`, Speculation Rules and WebGPU on Firefox ([webstatus.dev cross-document](https://api.webstatus.dev/v1/features?q=cross-document+view+transition); [webstatus.dev text-wrap-pretty](https://api.webstatus.dev/v1/features/text-wrap-pretty)). Anchor positioning is contested: web.dev declared it Baseline in January 2026 with Firefox 147, while webstatus.dev still returns "limited", and Safari 27 (17 September 2026) changed the `position-anchor` default from `auto` to `normal` and renamed the `anchor-valid`/`anchor-visible` keywords ([web.dev January 2026](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-01-2026); [webstatus.dev anchor-positioning](https://api.webstatus.dev/v1/features/anchor-positioning); [WebKit Safari 27.0](https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-for-safari-27-0/)). The practical tiering follows: use `:has()`, container queries, `color-mix()`, `@starting-style`, popover, `balance` and same-document view transitions freely; put scroll-driven animations behind `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())` because Firefox ESR and older Safari still lack them; declare `text-wrap: pretty` and `@view-transition { navigation: auto }` as harmless enhancements; wrap anchor-based UI in `@supports (anchor-name: --x)` with a fallback.

WebGL and WebGPU are a capability question, not a default. three.js is at **r187 (30 October 2026)**; WebGPURenderer with TSL is the actively developed path and falls back to WebGL 2 automatically, but a maintainer acknowledged r182 performance regressions fixed only in r183, so versions must be pinned ([three.js releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases); [three.js discourse](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/webgpu-significant-performance-drop-and-shadow-quality-regression-in-r182-vs-webgl-r170/89322); [threejs.org manual](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer)). WebGPU reaches about **88.6% of global usage**, with iOS Safari supporting it only from 26.0 and Firefox desktop and Android still disabled by default; a WebKit bug made WebGPU unusable on iOS 26.0–26.2 for at least one engine ([caniuse webgpu](https://caniuse.com/webgpu); [WebKit Bugzilla 301627](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=301627)). For a marketing site that means the WebGL path must look identical, not be an afterthought — and for a professional-services site the stronger inference is that a single full-screen quad shader (grain, a soft light gradient) in OGL 1.0.11 or raw WebGL buys "material" at a fraction of the cost of a 3D scene ([npm ogl](https://registry.npmjs.org/ogl/latest)). Rive canvas is at 2.49.4 and dotlottie-web at 0.82.1, with its WebGL/WebGPU backends vendor-described as production-ready despite the sub-1.0 registry version ([npm @rive-app/canvas](https://registry.npmjs.org/@rive-app/canvas/latest); [npm dotlottie-web](https://registry.npmjs.org/@lottiefiles/dotlottie-web/latest); [LottieFiles blog](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/hardware-accelerated-lottie-on-the-web-dotlottie-web-now-ships-webgl-webgpu)).

Build and QA tooling are equally pinned. **Vite 8.3.6** (Vite 8 GA 12 March 2026, Rolldown replacing esbuild and Rollup, Node 20.19+ or 22.12+) is the asset pipeline for a WordPress-delivered site; Astro 7.4.7 is relevant only if the front end is Astro-rendered ([npm vite](https://registry.npmjs.org/vite/latest); [Vite 8 announcement](https://vite.dev/blog/announcing-vite8); [Astro releases](https://github.com/withastro/astro/releases)). Motion is at 14.10.0 (5 November 2026); Playwright at 1.65 bundling Chromium 158, Firefox 159 and WebKit 27.2; **Lighthouse at 13.6.0 (27 October 2026)** and live in PageSpeed Insights by 2 November, which means lab scores collected before that date are not comparable to current ones and CrUX field data is the stable yardstick; axe-core at 4.14.0; chrome-devtools-mcp at 1.12.0 with the server and CLI officially stable since Chrome 149 ([motion.dev changelog](https://motion.dev/docs/changelog); [Playwright release notes](https://playwright.dev/docs/release-notes); [Lighthouse releases](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/releases); [relevantaudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/pagespeed-insights-lighthouse-13-6-lab-score-change/); [npm axe-core](https://registry.npmjs.org/axe-core/latest); [npm chrome-devtools-mcp](https://registry.npmjs.org/chrome-devtools-mcp/latest); [Chrome DevTools 149](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-devtools-149)). Core Web Vitals are unchanged — **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile**, mobile and desktop segmented — and the only 2026 addition is experimental soft-navigation variants in CrUX targeted to graduate in May 2027, which do not affect a WordPress site with full-page navigations; the "Engagement Reliability" metric circulating in SEO blogs has no Google source and the article promoting it still lists an FID target obsolete since 2024 ([web.dev vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals); [web.dev CrUX soft navs](https://web.dev/blog/crux-experimental-soft-navs/); [digivate](https://www.digivate.com/blog/seo/core-web-vitals-in-2026/)).

WordPress itself is at 7.1. **7.0 "Armstrong" shipped 20 May 2026** with the redesigned admin, a Command Palette, the AI Connectors API and a PHP 7.4 minimum; **7.1 "Mary Lou" shipped 19 August 2026** with responsive styles and pseudo-state styling in `theme.json`, a fully iframed post editor for all themes, a public SVG Icon API and speculative-loading constants; **7.2 is scheduled for 8 December 2026** with the "Ipsum" default theme ([raidboxes](https://raidboxes.io/en/blog/wordpress/wordpress-7-0/); [WordPress 7.1 announcement](https://wordpress.org/news/?p=21420); [WordPress 7.1 Field Guide](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2026/08/05/wordpress-7-1-field-guide); [Roadmap to 7.2](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2026/09/18/roadmap-to-7-2/)). The professional path for a hand-coded, no-page-builder theme is the **hybrid theme**: classic PHP templates plus a `theme.json` that supplies palette, typography and spacing presets so the client's Global Styles controls match the studio's tokens, with `block-template-parts` and `patterns/*.php` adopted selectively and stylesheets versioned with `filemtime()` ([WordPress developer blog: hybrid themes](https://developer.wordpress.org/news/2024/12/bridging-the-gap-hybrid-themes/)). The reference implementation of that pattern is the Roots stack — Sage 11.2 on Acorn 6, **PHP 8.3 minimum**, Vite 8 support and a Vite plugin that emits a dependency manifest for enqueuing and supports `theme.json` partials — which maps exactly onto a shared-hosting PHP 8.3 target such as Hostinger without needing Laravel ([Roots discourse April 2026](https://discourse.roots.io/t/wp-packages-updates-acorn-v6-and-vite-v8-support/30291); [Roots blog](https://roots.io/roots-vite-plugin-now-supports-theme-json-partials/)). Assets are enqueued as ES modules with `wp_enqueue_script_module()`, whose `$args` since 6.9 accept `fetchpriority` (use `high` for the hero and motion bundle), and classic scripts can declare `module_dependencies` since February 2026 ([wp_enqueue_script_module](https://developer.wordpress.org/reference/functions/wp_enqueue_script_module/); [Core changeset 61587](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/changeset/61587)). If the Interactivity API is used from PHP templates, output passes through `wp_interactivity_process_directives()` ([wp-trac #61387](https://lists.wordpress.org/pipermail/wp-trac/2024-June/522590.html)).

Two WordPress-specific hazards need measured handling. Global-styles bleed can no longer be stopped by `wp_dequeue_style('global-styles')` alone: core now generates the inline CSS in the footer and moves it into the head, so Trac #65336 is unresolved and the working pattern is to remove `wp_enqueue_global_styles` from `wp_footer` at priority 1 and then dequeue `wp-block-library`, `wp-block-library-theme`, `classic-theme-styles` and `global-styles` late — verified on the live HTML, not on the source ([Trac #65336](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/65336); [wp-trac #64358](https://lists.wordpress.org/pipermail/wp-trac/2025-December/554451.html); [code.dplugins](https://code.dplugins.com/remove-the-gutenberg-block-library-css-from-wordpress/)). The safer architecture scopes the theme's CSS under `@layer` and a root class so core's inline styles cannot win specificity fights. Speculative loading shipped in 6.8 and 7.1 added `WP_SPECULATIVE_LOADING_DEFAULT_MODE` and `WP_SPECULATIVE_LOADING_DEFAULT_EAGERNESS` as wp-config constants; with LiteSpeed full-page cache on Hostinger most pages are static at the edge, so raising to `moderate` is low-risk on a brochure site while staying `conservative` on pages with forms ([Speculative Loading in 6.8](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2025/03/06/speculative-loading-in-6-8/); [365i analysis](https://www.365i.co.uk/news/2026/08/20/wordpress-7-1-speculative-loading-hosting/)). LiteSpeed Cache is at 7.9.3 (3 November 2026) and is pre-installed on Hostinger; 2026 settings guidance is to leave CSS/JS combine off under HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, enable WebP replacement and "Viewport Images" so above-the-fold imagery escapes lazy-loading, and keep ESI off unless needed ([wordpress.org LiteSpeed Cache](https://wordpress.org/plugins/litespeed-cache/); [Hostinger LiteSpeed tutorial](https://www.hostinger.com/uk/tutorials/wordpress-litespeed-website-optimization-tool/); [n6cloud guide](https://n6cloud.com/blog/speed-up-wordpress-litespeed-cache/)).

Font delivery for Hebrew has clear rules even where the exact `unicode-range` string could not be confirmed. `unicode-range` only tells the browser which subset file to fetch; the size reduction must come from pre-subsetting, and variable fonts require WOFF2 ([FontCreator tutorial](https://www.high-logic.com/en/font-editor/fontcreator/tutorials/improve-font-performance-with-character-set-subsetting); [Walter Ebert](https://walterebert.com/blog/subsetting-web-fonts)). web.dev's guidance is to inline `@font-face` in `<head>`, preload sparingly because preload ignores `unicode-range`, mix `font-display: swap` for branding type with `optional` for body, and use `size-adjust` with `ascent-override`, `descent-override` and `line-gap-override` to cut CLS — values that depend on the specific web-font and fallback pair ([web.dev font best practices](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices); [Chrome improved font fallbacks](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/font-fallbacks)). The W3C list discussion that Arabic "will end up tiny in comparison to the English" when metric harmonisation is missing is the warning that these heuristics were designed around Latin; Hebrew fallback metrics must be checked visually against a Hebrew-capable system font ([public-css-archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2024Mar/0520.html)). Fontsource ships variable Noto Sans Hebrew (wght 100–900) and Heebo as WOFF2 with `tech(variations)` and a separate Hebrew subset ([Fontsource Noto Sans Hebrew variable](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@fontsource-variable/noto-sans-hebrew@5.2.8/README.md); [Fontsource Heebo](https://fontsource.org/fonts/heebo/cdn)).

## Motion craft is masked lines, compositor transitions and a designed reduced state

The signature choreography of 2026 winners, inferred from the score pages rather than stated by any jury, runs: a gate or unlock gesture before the scene begins; one hero object or terrain with inertial easing; scroll moving the camera rather than DOM elements; text appearing as masked line reveals; the cursor used as a reveal lens rather than a decorative trailer ([Awwwards: Why Zero](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/why-zero); [Awwwards: Hubtown](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/hubtown); [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)). For a professional-services site the tired list matters more than the winners list. Scroll-triggered fade-in is called one of the most ubiquitous and most user-hostile patterns, with the rule "if a button needs to be there, it is there from the start" ([datadaily](https://datadaily.pages.dev/scroll-fade-ux-antipattern)); NN/g's Katie Sherwin holds that even subtle parallax carries the usability problems of the flashier versions, and the academic literature finds parallax works through perceived "coolness", not content ([Fast Company on NN/g](https://www.fastcompany.com/90309395/why-parallax-scrolling-needs-to-die); [Memphis](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/9564)); Eric Bailey's argument against custom cursors is that a low-vision user who needs a large high-contrast cursor can lose it entirely — "a hard stop" ([Eric Bailey](https://ericwbailey.design/writing/dont-use-custom-css-mouse-cursors)). Anthropic's own frontend-design skill now reads like a negative-space list that converges on the same verdict: "ideally one orchestrated moment such as a page-load sequence", and no fade-and-slide-up on every section or hover transitions on every card ([frontend-design SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)).

The concrete numbers in circulation come from library docs and design-system token scales, not from award pages. GSAP's reference masked reveal is `type: "words,lines"`, `mask: "lines"`, `autoSplit: true`, lines from `yPercent: 100` and `opacity: 0` over **0.6 s with `expo.out` and a 0.1 s stagger**, created inside `onSplit()` after `document.fonts.ready` so the tween survives re-splits ([GSAP SplitText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/)). Design-system scales run from **100 ms** (pressed states, quick exits) and 150 ms (interactive highlights) at Atlassian to 240 ms for expansions, 400 ms slow and 700 ms for background dimming at Carbon ([Atlassian motion](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion); [Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). Token practice is to specify duration, easing and delay together, keep four to six duration values, and write a reduced-motion override (duration-instant) into the spec ([dev.to motion tokens](https://dev.to/raxxostudios/motion-design-tokens-that-actually-compose-durations-easings-choreography-12e4)). No public 2026 studio motion spec or choreography table surfaced from Resn, Active Theory, Locomotive, Immersive Garden, Unseen, Lusion, Obys or their peers; the table format (trigger, what moves, token refs, stagger, scroll range) is an inference from the "specify together" guidance.

Text splitting changed status in 2026. Adrian Roselli's February audit found SplitText's default `aria-label`/`aria-hidden` scheme fails in **JAWS/Chrome (never announced), Narrator/Edge (first letter only) and VoiceOver/Safari (a subset of letters, read one by one)**, and his advice is to "find another method" until GSAP issue #642 is addressed ([Adrian Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/02/you-know-what-just-dont-split-words-into-letters.html)). GSAP's own a11y page concedes the default drops nested links and recommends `aria: false` with a screen-reader-only duplicate ([GSAP a11y](https://www.gsap.com/resources/a11y/)). The defensible pattern for a services site is therefore lines only, masked, animating the line box, `aria: "none"` with the real heading kept in the DOM as the accessible copy, `autoSplit` for resize, and a revert after the intro so the DOM returns to plain text. For Hebrew the inference is stronger still: character splitting would separate final-letter forms and nikud combining marks, and an `xPercent` slide must be mirrored (positive x enters from the right in reading order) while `yPercent` is direction-neutral. No source addresses splitting Hebrew; the 3.15.1 RTL line-break fix is the only direct evidence.

Page transitions have a native default. Cross-document view transitions are production-ready in Chromium 126+ and Safari 18.2+ and are an Interop 2026 focus area; Google Search ships them for AI Mode switches, where auto-generated keyframes dropped frames on older devices until rewritten to run on the compositor ([Chrome: Google AI Mode case study](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/google-ai-mode-view-transitions-case-study); [WebKit: Interop 2026](https://webkit.org/?p=17818)). The August 2026 practitioner argument is that a Barba + GSAP + Lenis router adds **about 180 kB of libraries and ~750 lines of code**, less than a third of which is animation, while the native approach keeps back/forward, scroll restoration, analytics pageviews, focus and screen-reader announcements, and switches off with `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { @view-transition { navigation: none; } }` ([usecue](https://www.usecue.com/blog/page-transitions-done-right/)). Barba remains justified only where a persistent WebGL canvas or audio must survive navigation ([Codrops Barba + Astro](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/08/creating-custom-page-transitions-in-astro-with-barba-js-and-gsap/)). Astro's `slide` directive is defined as out-left/in-from-right; in a Hebrew site the forward convention reverses, so slides must be mirrored or replaced by a direction-neutral clip or fade — an inference, since no source addresses RTL transitions ([Astro docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/)).

Material in 2026 is grain over photography, not glass over motion. Grain is framed as a tactile counter to over-polished imagery, best implemented as lightweight CSS gradients or small compressed assets ([Bubble trends](https://bubble.io/blog/web-design-trends/)); glass is described as the dominant UI direction because of Apple's Liquid Glass and simultaneously as something designers hope "will pass" because it "goes against accessibility too much" ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends); [Lyssna survey](https://www.lyssna.com/blog/ux-design-trends-for-2024/)). Chrome's own warning is that CPU throttling does not touch the GPU process, so blur filters and box-shadows that look fine on desktop run far slower on a phone — a 270 ms throttled interaction measured 620 ms on a real mid-tier device with ~300 ms of GPU work ([Chrome: DevTools grounded in real-world data](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/devtools-grounded-real-world)). Scrubbing `<video>` is unreliable on mobile Safari; frame sequences give control but decoding dominates cost, so OPTIKKA served a device-specific sequence per breakpoint and the standard is a poster that matches frame 1 exactly, acts as the LCP candidate, and is what Low Power Mode and reduced-motion users receive ([Codrops OPTIKKA](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/16/creating-smooth-scroll-synchronized-animation-for-optikka-from-html5-video-to-frame-sequences/)).

Reduced motion is a second design, not a switch. The 2026 consensus — GSAP, Webflow, web.dev, Smashing — is "reduce, don't remove": keep functional motion as a gentle crossfade, remove decorative loops, parallax and large swipes, and offer an in-page toggle because users do not all know the OS setting ([GSAP a11y](https://www.gsap.com/resources/a11y/); [Webflow: motion and accessibility](https://webflow.com/blog/motion-and-accessibility); [web.dev prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)). WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires pause/stop/hide for auto-playing motion over five seconds regardless of the media query, so an autoplaying hero video on a services site needs a visible pause control; 2.3.3 (AAA) asks that interaction-triggered motion be disableable ([W3C Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide); [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions)). The performance gate that matches Chrome's documentation is no longer a bare "4× CPU": Chrome 134+ calibrates **"low-tier" and "mid-tier mobile" presets** for the tester's machine, recommends mid-tier, and with CrUX enabled suggests throttling that matches the site's 75th-percentile users; Long Animation Frames (Chrome 123+) replace Long Tasks for INP attribution ([Chrome DevTools performance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/performance); [Chrome: LoAF](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/long-animation-frames)). Practitioner budgets of **JS under 200 kB and Lighthouse performance above 90** are stricter than anything Awwwards publishes — its Mobile Excellence checklist still uses Lighthouse 2–3 era metric names — and therefore the more defensible target ([dev.to speed techniques](https://dev.to/imran_khan_a3cc224344dbcf/website-speed-optimization-15-techniques-that-work-in-2026-3beb); [Awwwards mobile excellence PDF](https://www.awwwards.com/mobile-excellence-guidelines.pdf)).

## Agent-built design reaches the bar when the direction is written before the code

The 2026 toolchain for agent-run design has become official and, tellingly, negative. Anthropic rewrote its `frontend-design` skill twice (9 June and 3 September 2026) specifically "to avoid generic design defaults"; the current version names five clusters to avoid when the brief is open — warm cream with a high-contrast serif and terracotta accent, near-black with an acid-green or vermilion accent, the hairline-rule "broadsheet", the "SaaS card kit" and "template chrome" (tracked all-caps eyebrows, middle-dot meta strings, monospace data labels, arrows appended to links) — and its method is "plan, review, then build" with a compact token system of four to six colours, type roles and ASCII wireframes, spending boldness "in one place" ([GitHub commits](https://api.github.com/repos/anthropics/skills/commits?path=skills/frontend-design&per_page=5); [frontend-design SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). Impeccable is at **v4.8.1 (6–7 November 2026)** with 24 commands and a 62-rule deterministic detector that scans edited UI files and returns findings before the agent reports done; its named defaults to kill are "Inter for everything", "purple-to-blue gradients", nested cards and a rounded-square icon above every heading, and its author's rule is that "a single section reviewed closely beats a whole page reviewed at a glance" ([impeccable.style changelog](https://impeccable.style/changelog); [Impeccable README](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)). Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines ship as a live-fetched agent command returning terse file:line findings ([Vercel changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/web-interface-guidelines-now-available-as-an-agent-command)); GSAP publishes eight official skills ([gsap-skills](https://github.com/greensock/gsap-skills)); Mobbin's MCP (launched 11 May 2026, docs updated 31 October) exposes 621,500+ screens through `search_screens`, `search_flows`, `search_sections` and collection tools at 60 requests per minute ([mobbin.com/mcp.md](https://mobbin.com/mcp.md); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260511053592/en/Mobbin-Launches-MCP-Server-Giving-AI-Tools-621500-Real-App-Screens-to-Reference)); Chrome DevTools MCP 1.12.0 adds `get_css_style` (1.10) and a `lighthouse_audit` that returns failing audits with DOM nodes inline (1.11) ([chrome-devtools-mcp releases](https://github.com/ChromeDevTools/chrome-devtools-mcp/releases)). The DTCG token format reached its first stable version, 2025.10, on 28 October 2025, but Style Dictionary 5 still lists full 2025.10 support as in progress and Tokens Studio's native export is a multi-set envelope rather than a flat file, so token pipelines must be pinned and validated ([designtokens.org](https://www.designtokens.org/); [Style Dictionary DTCG](https://styledictionary.com/info/dtcg/); [Penpot community](https://community.penpot.app/t/token-export-is-tokens-studio-format-not-dtcg-2025-10/10544)).

The critics' diagnosis of the quality ceiling is consistent and specific. The model returns "distributional convergence" — Inter, violet gradients, shadcn blocks, hero-plus-three-cards — and every source that reports getting past it credits a written direction and reference set, not a better model ([Mowgli](https://mowgli.ai/blog/claude-code-ui-design-workflow); [Joshua Snoddy](https://www.joshuasnoddy.com/blog/why-ai-websites-look-the-same/)). Nielsen Norman Group found AI prototyping tools "often produced unpolished, indistinctive visual styles" while "a detailed prompt produced results that closely matched the human-designed version" ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/ai-prototyping/)). The Codrops/Diorama piece on design systems with agents lists the limits that matter for a studio: agents' motion timing is unreliable, their accessibility checks are stricter than humans' but less visually compelling, unsupervised output "feels empty", and raw hex on a design sheet is a design bug — "treat a design sheet as a contract, not a picture" ([knowledge-base intake of Codrops](https://github.com/adamforrester/knowledge-base/issues/46)). Meng To's acceptance checklist adds the asset rules: original or licensed imagery only, photographs for every avatar, no model-authored SVG/CSS/canvas illustrations, no generated people presented as real, and "Awwwards quality is an acceptance bar, never an award claim" ([AgenticSkills](https://agenticskills.io/skills/build-awwwards-quality-sites)).

The workflow that credible 2026 write-ups converge on is design package first, one builder, options only at the direction stage. Mowgli's sequence is to inventory screens and states in plan mode, "compare three distinct directions, then choose one" — directions that differ in typography, palette, density and layout structure, "not just color swaps" — record tokens, components, ban list, required states and a screenshot-based definition of done in CLAUDE.md, install the frontend-design skill only after a direction is chosen, and build one screen at a time against a concrete target ([Mowgli](https://mowgli.ai/blog/claude-code-ui-design-workflow)). Impeccable's `shape`, `init` and `document` commands produce PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md before code; Diorama keeps tokens as a versioned package "because they must be identical everywhere" and separates Drafts pages from Handoff pages ([Impeccable README](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable); [knowledge-base intake](https://github.com/adamforrester/knowledge-base/issues/46)). Parallel builders are viable only with worktree or route isolation and a shared token package; Claude Code's agent teams lack worktree isolation and the docs warn that "running several sessions or subagents at once multiplies token usage" ([code.claude.com agents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents)). No source documents an "Art Director" agent with measured outcomes; in practice the role is the human or lead agent who writes the package, picks the direction and judges evidence, and Anthropic's governance line — "an agent's approval never counts as a person's" — is the analogue ([claude.dev blog](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)).

The render–critique–refine loop has a best-evidenced shape. Screenshot each state at two viewports (about **390 and 1440 px**) with Playwright MCP, Chrome DevTools MCP or Claude in Chrome; compare against the target (a Figma frame, a reference image, or a pixel diff) and produce an element/expected/actual/fix table; read computed CSS through `evaluate` or `get_css_style` because screenshots do not expose it; cap at about three rounds before a human looks ([Mowgli](https://mowgli.ai/blog/claude-code-ui-design-workflow); [Figma-to-React pipeline](https://medium.com/@aliafsah1988/how-to-turn-claude-code-into-a-figma-to-react-pipeline-that-visually-verifies-its-own-work-030246f600a9)). Playwright's `toHaveScreenshot()` emits expected/actual/diff images with typical tolerances of 0.5–2% ([Steve Kinney](https://stevekinney.com/courses/self-testing-ai-agents/visual-regression-as-a-feedback-loop)); Diorama goes further with "neuro-symbolic validation" — design geometry exported as JSON and checked arithmetically against browser layout, "because pixel-diff tolerances can hide 1px drift" ([knowledge-base intake](https://github.com/adamforrester/knowledge-base/issues/46)). The only hard efficiency number comes from Anthropic's code review, not design: a fresh-context reviewer and evidence requirements raised first-round PR pass rates from **65% to 78%** and cut mean reviews to pass from 2.08 to 1.32 ([claude.dev blog](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)). The inference is a three-layer gate: deterministic lint on save (Impeccable detector, Vercel guidelines, axe); measured checks (computed CSS, geometry, Lighthouse, pixel diff at ≤ 2%); a fresh-context critic that sees only evidence at two viewports plus reduced motion, scored against a written rubric. Motion must be judged by screencast or trace, not stills — Chrome DevTools MCP's ffmpeg-free screencast is the instrument — and reference intake must be a written extraction (what it does, why it is level, which tokens or structure to borrow) because the one practitioner report on raw Mobbin screenshots says they yield "generic, vibe-coded results unless forced to analyze the screens first" ([Evangeline Ng](https://evangelineng.substack.com/p/mobbin-mcp-claude-code-generate-prototypes)).

Imagery has a priced stack. On fal.ai as of 9 November 2026: FLUX.2 at **$0.012/MP** and FLUX.2 Flash at $0.005/MP for photoreal scenes, FLUX 3 edit at $0.048/MP, Nano Banana 2.1 token-billed at roughly $0.040 (1K), $0.059 (2K) and $0.134 (4K) with an edit endpoint taking up to 14 reference images and system prompts for persistent style, Recraft V4 at $0.04 for brand and vector work, Bria RMBG 2.0 at $0.018 for background removal and SeedVR2 at $0.001/MP for upscaling; no latency figures are published ([fal.ai/pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing); [fal Nano Banana 2.1](https://fal.ai/models/google/nano-banana-2.1); [fal Recraft V4](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/recraft/v4/text-to-image)). For a licensed professional (for example a lawyer, an appraiser or an accountant) the stronger rule is the critics' one: real photography of the practitioner, the office and the real subject of the work (for an appraiser, the built environment he values) beats any generated scene, and generated imagery is confined to abstract material (texture, light) where no claim of reality is made.

## Hebrew type, RTL craft and Israeli law set hard constraints that the local market mostly ignores

The Hebrew typeface bench is deep enough to avoid the two local defaults (Assistant on Elementor, Heebo everywhere). Google Fonts' Hebrew set, verified through the Fontsource API, lists **62 families**; the variable ones include Heebo, Rubik, Assistant, Frank Ruhl Libre, Noto Sans Hebrew (with a width axis), Noto Serif Hebrew, Noto Rashi Hebrew and Miriam Libre, while IBM Plex Sans Hebrew and David Libre remain static ([Fontsource API, subsets=hebrew](https://api.fontsource.org/v1/fonts?subsets=hebrew); [Fontsource Noto Sans Hebrew](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-hebrew/about)). The premium bar is commercial: AlefAlefAlef (Ploni, Almoni, Anomalia, Mugrabi) licenses webfonts per domain capped at one million monthly page views, VAT-exclusive, with trial fonts on request ([AlefAlefAlef FAQ](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/resources/faq/)); Fontef carries the Narkiss family, Hebrew companions for Lyon, Canela, Graphik and Publico, and "Variable Classics" with weight + optical size (Frank Rühl), width + round (Haim), weight + width (Aharoni) and weight + monospace (Miriam) axes ([fontef.com](https://fontef.com/)); Fontimonim lists 75 families "from ₪245 per weight", most likely a desktop starting price ([fontimonim.co.il](https://fontimonim.co.il/)); Typotheque's Hebrew programme covers 21 families ([Typotheque](https://typotheque.com/blog/a-new-hebrew-font-collection)). No foundry publishes a flat web-licence price list. The credible pairings for a premium professional-services site are an all-free stack (Frank Ruhl Libre variable serif for display with Heebo, Assistant or Noto Sans Hebrew for text, or IBM Plex Sans Hebrew for a cooler register) or one commercial display face (Narkiss Block or Text, Lyon HLAR, Canela HLAR, Ploni, Almoni, Mugrabi, Oron Classic) with a free text face — the pattern drukerlaw.co.il instantiates with Ploni and Miriam Libre.

Hebrew type craft has structural reasons for its numbers. Hebrew has one ascender, lamed, and few descenders, so a Hebrew face at the same nominal size reads smaller and denser than Latin; Dalton Maag set Ubuntu's Hebrew body height at 90% of cap height after finding x-height-level Hebrew "too small to stand up against the Latin", and Dinamo's Favorit Hebrew ships an axis to scale Hebrew up to Latin cap height ([itsbaba: Hebrew fonts compared](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts); [Canonical blog](https://canonical.com/blog/intial-hebrew-trials); [ABC Dinamo: Favorit Hebrew](https://abcdinamo.com/typefaces/favorit-hebrew)). Myriad Hebrew's designer made Latin and Hebrew figures equal height because Latin figures are too tall for Hebrew text ([Typekit specimen](https://blog.typekit.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/myriad-hebrew-online-specimen.pdf)). web.dev's Hebrew-language guide gives 45–75 characters per line with 66 as ideal ([web.dev Typography (HE)](https://web.dev/learn/design/typography?hl=he)); vendor rules of thumb put Hebrew body line-height at 1.6–2.0 and headlines at 1.1–1.3 ([fontygen](https://fontygen.com/font-generator/hebrew/)). The inferred 2026 defaults — body one step above a Latin default (about 17–19 px equivalent), line-height about 1.6–1.7, measure about 55–65 Hebrew characters, a sturdy regular or medium rather than light weights, digits height-matched to the Hebrew body or isolated with `<bdi>` and nudged via `:lang(en)` — are inferences from glyph structure, not published numbers; no authoritative Hebrew web-typography guideline with numeric ratios exists. `text-wrap: balance` is script-agnostic and safe on Hebrew headlines; `pretty` is a progressive enhancement with no Firefox support ([savvy.co.il text-wrap](https://savvy.co.il/en/blog/css/css-text-wrap-pretty-balance/)).

RTL implementation has a W3C-backed recipe and a local market that has not adopted it. `dir="rtl"` belongs on `<html>` in markup, never via CSS, "because direction information should be part of the markup so it survives when CSS is unavailable"; logical properties everywhere; `dir="auto"` and `<bdi>` for inserted or user text; `dir="auto"` on inputs (right-aligns Hebrew, left-aligns English) and `dirname` to submit the computed direction ([W3C i18n: structural markup and RTL](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir)). Mozilla's own guidelines reserve `:dir(rtl)` for cases with no logical equivalent and note `text-align: match-parent` for LTR text embedded in RTL ([Firefox RTL guidelines](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/code-quality/coding-style/rtl_guidelines.html)). WordPress.org re-implemented a theme's RTL overrides with logical properties in December 2024 even as WPML still documents `rtl.css`, and drukerlaw.co.il still ships compiled `*.rtl.css` per breakpoint ([meta.trac changeset 14308](https://meta.trac.wordpress.org/changeset/14308); [WPML](https://wpml.org/documentation/theme-compatibility/build-rtl-wordpress-themes/); [drukerlaw.co.il](https://www.drukerlaw.co.il/)). GSAP ScrollTrigger has no native RTL mode; the forum fix for reversed horizontal pins is `flex-direction: row-reverse` or `direction: rtl` with tweens from the end value to 0, and `dir="rtl"` on a scroll container breaks ScrollToPlugin's centring math ([GSAP forum 40221](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/40221-horizontal-scroll-but-it-is-reversed/); [GSAP forum RTL tag](https://gsap.com/community/tags/rtl/)). The inference is to prefer transform-based tracks with positive x over native horizontal scroll containers, whose `scrollLeft` sign conventions differ across engines in RTL, and to mirror snap indicators and progress bars.

Israeli accessibility law reaches a small professional-services site. The duty comes from the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law through the Service Accessibility Regulations 2013 (Regulation 35), implemented via **IS 5568 Part 1 at Level AA**, legally anchored to WCAG 2.0 even though the 2023 edition tracks 2.1 more closely; no WCAG 2.2 adoption was found ([Kol Zchut: web accessibility](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות); [SII 5568 PDF](https://www.isoc.org.il/files/docs/5568.pdf); [user-a.co.il](https://user-a.co.il/en/standards/israeli-accessibility-standards-vs-wcag)). Per Kol Zchut (updated 26 August 2026), a sole trader registered as עוסק פטור is fully exempt from web adjustments and an average turnover under **₪120,000** gives a renewable three-year exemption, but commercial blogs still quote ₪100,000 and the primary regulation text was not fetched, so the threshold is contested; every source agrees that **an accessibility statement is mandatory even for an exempt site**, covering how the site was made accessible, how to operate it, what was not made accessible and alternatives, and how to contact the operator ([Kol Zchut: exemptions](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פטור_מחובת_הנגשה_לאתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות); [vee.co.il](https://vee.co.il/exemption-from-website-accessibility/)). Enforcement runs through a 60-day cure notice, civil compensation up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage, Commission orders with ₪7,500/day fines and class actions ([Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הנגשת_אתרי_אינטרנט_ואפליקציות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות)). No Commission position on overlay widgets exists; the FTC's 2025 action against accessiBe is the best-documented regulatory item, and the premium Israeli sites all load UserWay anyway ([FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/complaint.222_3156_-_accessibe_final_consent_package_without_signatures.pdf)). An overlay is defensible only as an add-on to a natively conformant build, never as the conformance itself.

Privacy law reaches the contact form. **Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law has been in force since 14 August 2025**; the section-11 notice at the point of collection now has five items — whether providing data is mandatory or voluntary and the consequence of refusal, the purpose, the controller's name and contact, recipients and purposes of disclosure, and the rights to inspect and correct — and "בעל שליטה" replaces "בעל מאגר" ([alaw.co.il: Amendment 13](https://alaw.co.il/privacy/law/amendment-13/)). The Authority has already fined a small company **₪12,000** for collecting personal data through an online form without the full notice, in a case where the privacy policy was also unavailable on the site ([Chamber of Commerce legal update](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/163610/)). Its final opinion on consent (25 February 2026) says meeting section 11 is a minimum and does not by itself make consent informed ([Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/?p=24351)). A sole practitioner's contact-form data needs no DPO or registration (none of the four DPO categories; far below the 10,000 and 100,000 thresholds); the claim that Amendment 13 requires an active cookie-consent pop-up appears only on a web-agency blog and not in the law-firm sources.

Israeli service conventions and search complete the picture. The pattern observed in the field is a header WhatsApp link (`wa.me/972…`, international format), `tel:` CTAs, a short lead form, a Waze deep link (`https://waze.com/ul?ll=…&navigate=yes`) beside Google Maps, and footer links to privacy, terms and the accessibility statement ([drukerlaw.co.il](https://www.drukerlaw.co.il/); [Google Developers: Waze deep links](https://developers.google.com/waze/api)). Bit and PayBox are P2P rails with business tiers, not web checkout, and belong on the invoice, not the site ([Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/finances-news/Article-3a2f2532b660a91027.htm)). Google is roughly 98% of Israeli search (vendor claim) and users mix Hebrew and English in queries; a Google Business Profile needs name, address with building number and phone identical to the site ([Argos Multilingual](https://www.argosmultilingual.com/blog/hebrew-seo); [inn.co.il](https://www.inn.co.il/news/695315)). Google's structured-data guidance is to use the most specific `LocalBusiness` subtype with `name`, `address`, `geo` at five decimals, `telephone` with country code, `openingHoursSpecification`, `url` and `priceRange`; drukerlaw emits only generic Yoast `Organization`/`WebSite` nodes ([Google Search Central: Local Business](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). For a regulated profession the licence belongs on the page; real-estate appraisal is the example researched: appraisal is a licensed profession under the Real Estate Appraisers Law 2001, and the Appraisers' Chamber has complained about valuation by unlicensed websites, which makes a prominently stated licence number a trust and compliance signal ([Midrag](https://www.midrag.co.il/Content/Tip/7980); [Nadlan Center](https://www.nadlancenter.co.il/tags/לשכת_שמאי_המקרקעין)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes "award-tier" for a professional-services site. The bar that matters is not the 8.09 WebGL story but the developer-jury profile that only Cartier achieved in the window — transitions and accessibility passing together — because a services site cannot buy its way to a jury's Creativity score and does not need to: the local professional-services tier is interchangeable template work, and a Hebrew-only site with real typographic hierarchy, one spent moment of motion, native accessibility and a measured 60 fps pass on a mid-range phone would already be singular in its category. The second shift is that craft in 2026 is defined as much by what is withheld as by what is added. Every critic, juror and skill author converged on the same ban list — scroll-fade on every block, parallax, custom cursors, glass over motion, bento by default, character-split headlines, preloaders that delay content — and on the same positives: a subject-derived visual thesis, two colours, lines of text that rise once from under a mask, a poster that is the LCP, and a reduced-motion state designed rather than switched off.

The third shift is methodological. The strongest 2026 practice treats the written design package as the contract and the agent as a builder who must prove each claim with evidence a different reader judges — a fresh-context critic, a deterministic detector, a computed style, a frame trace. For a Hebrew RTL WordPress site this is not optional overhead: no source addresses Hebrew text splitting, RTL page transitions or RTL ScrollTrigger behaviour, so every one of those must be verified on the effective value in Safari and Chrome rather than assumed from Latin documentation. The gap between the local market and the international bar is wide enough that a disciplined studio can clear it, and narrow enough in law (IS 5568, Amendment 13) that it must.
